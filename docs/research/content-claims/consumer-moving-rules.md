**Main corrections at a glance.**
1. FMCSA's 2022 final rule (87 FR 24431, effective 2022-06-27) rewrote much of Part 375. Guides written before then are out of date on surveys, the order for service, the bill of lading and when the booklets are handed over (A1, C2, C11, C12).
2. The 50-mile survey rule is gone. Movers must survey every interstate shipment, on-site or by video, unless the shopper waives the survey in writing (C2). Compliance facts §2's summary of the broker rule ("a physical survey by the carrier where the goods are within 50 miles") predates this change.
3. The order for service no longer exists. The bill of lading must reach the shopper at least 3 days before loading, and the shopper may rescind it within 3 days of signing (C12).
4. The 110% rule is confirmed. FMCSA guidance says the mover bills the rest no sooner than 30 days after delivery. The sources found don't say the balance is "due within 30 days" (C6).
5. "Binding not-to-exceed" is not a federal term (C7). No federal rule was found that requires weight-only pricing (C9), addresses hourly pricing (C19) or caps deposits (C17).
6. FMCSA's red-flag wording speaks of an on-site inspection, not "on-site or virtual". Its USDOT-number advice sits on the tips page, not in the red-flag excerpts found (C14).
7. BLS OEWS, May 2025: $19.35 an hour median for SOC 53-7062, via O*NET. BLS's own page gives the matching $40,240 a year (C20).

| C18 | Extra (accessorial) services such as packing, stair ("flight") carries, long carries, shuttles and elevators are priced in the mover's tariff. The mover must ask about and settle these charges before preparing the bill of lading; if it doesn't, it must deliver the goods and may bill the extra charges only after 30 days. ## Items for counsel

These are questions. The note draws no conclusion on any of them.

1. **Ranges and estimates.** Part 375 and 49 CFR 371.113 govern estimates by carriers and household goods brokers, and FMCSA lists phone or online "sight unseen" estimates as a red flag (C14). Do AMC's planning ranges, calculator results or a "recent request mix" block raise any issue under these rules, and do ST-6 and ST-7 label them well enough? Compliance facts §2 asks the same question.
2. **The 110% rule.** The regulation sets the 110% delivery cap (375.407, 375.703). The "at least 30 days" deferral for the balance appears in Appendix A, the FMCSA handbook and FMCSA's Subpart D page, not in the regulation text found (C6). How should How movers charge word the balance and its timing?
3. **FMCSA pages that lag the regulation.** Some FMCSA consumer pages still use pre-2022 wording (the 50-mile survey rule, the order for service), and the red-flag page speaks of on-site inspections while 375.103 counts video surveys (C2, C12, C14). When FMCSA guidance and the current regulation differ, which should the pages quote, and how should they note the difference?
4. **"Binding not-to-exceed."** Federal rules define only binding and non-binding estimates (C7). May pages describe "not-to-exceed" as an industry term some movers use, and should they mention California's state-defined "Not to Exceed Price"?
5. **Valuation numbers.** Can pages state the 60-cents-per-pound released rate and the full-value default as fixed figures, given that the STB order lets carriers index some figures and each mover's tariff prices full value protection (C10, A6)? Is the 2012 order, RR 999 (Amendment No. 5), still current?
6. **Interstate versus local labels.** Federal rules turn on whether a move is interstate, including the routing rule and the commercial-zone exception, not on distance (C16). AMC's cost model uses a local radius (`{{setting.local_radius_miles}}`, 50 miles). How should the cost pages and the calculator label local, in-state long-distance and interstate moves so readers don't infer which rules apply from AMC's distance bands?
7. **Hourly pricing.** Part 375 is silent on hourly rates, and states set local pricing rules; California allows hourly pricing under 100 miles for moves within the state (C19). How should How movers charge describe hourly pricing on interstate moves?
8. **Deposits.** No federal cap on movers' deposits was found. FMCSA calls a large deposit a red flag without giving a number, and only brokers must disclose deposit and refund policies (C17). What may the deposits section say beyond quoting FMCSA?
9. **State licensing of interstate movers.** California issues an Interstate Mover license to companies that move goods only into or out of California (C16). Does that affect the statement that states regulate in-state moves, or the screening described on How we verify movers?
10. **Blank-document guidance.** FMCSA's blank-document warning rests on 2011 guidance (76 FR 50537) that interprets 375.501(d), a section the 2022 rule removed (A8). Can pages still cite the warning as current?
11. **Arbitration wording.** Movers must offer arbitration programs (C13), while implications §3.2 keeps arbitration out of AMC's consent box. How should pages describe movers' programs so readers don't take them for AMC's own dispute terms?
12. **Booklet links.** Movers must hand over "Ready to Move?" and "Your Rights and Responsibilities" with the estimate (C11). If AMC pages link both and describe that duty, what wording avoids implying that AMC supplies them on movers' behalf?
