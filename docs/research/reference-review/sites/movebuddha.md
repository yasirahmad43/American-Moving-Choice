# moveBuddha: reference review

A moving research and review site that also runs lead paths [capture /moving-cost-calculator/; walk], and one of the owner's 11 references, useful mainly for its no-contact calculator and data-led city pages [inference].

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-27. **Evidence:** captured 14 of 14 pages; guided walks of the calculator (prices with no contact details), its results page, the "Mover Marketplace" first screen and one partner quote form up to its email step; Semrush US, October 2026; single-run WebSearch that was not re-checked, so search-only ownership, legal and competitor-program claims are left out. **Confidence:** medium, because no consent wording, privacy policy, terms or mover-facing page was seen, though the pages and pre-contact flows are well captured.

## At a glance
- It calls itself "a moving research and review platform" but runs partner quote forms, a "Mover Marketplace" and a specialist-call bid path [capture /moving-cost-calculator/; walk /marketplace/, /moving-cost-calculator-tool/].
- Its disclosure says it "may receive compensation" for clicks, webform submissions or calls, and names neither the payer nor the pricing model [capture /advertising-disclosure/].
- The calculator shows three price ranges before any contact field and is the top organic page, at about 4,399 visits a month (11.47%) [walk; semrush top_pages].
- The widget and its results page priced the same move differently, and other prices and counts drift between pages [walk; capture].
- National partner cards with superlatives, ratings and phone numbers recur on geo, route and guide pages, with no "sponsored" label in the captured text [capture].
- No walked path reached consent wording [walk].
- About 38,335 US organic visits a month, Authority Score 44 and 9,427 referring domains [semrush domain_rank, backlinks_overview].

- Good: the partner form asks move details first, accepts answers from people without an exact date and shows three labeled stages before any contact field [walk, partner form].

[inference] The two sit uneasily, but the real count is unknown.
- Inconsistent: for Atlanta 30303 to Miami 33101 (2-bedroom apartment), the widget's professional range was $3,044–$8,893 and the results page's $2,455–$5,126; freight's top fell from $7,814 to $1,761 [walk /moving-cost-calculator/, /moving-cost-calculator-tool/].

## Mover side
No captured page is aimed at movers, and the footer labels include no mover sign-up link, so joining, fees, sharing caps, exclusivity and lead checks are unseen [capture]. Partner cards show toll-free numbers while local movers' cards show local ones, and the Safeway card's number is also the partner form's "call us" line [capture /movers/ri/, /movers/ca/; walk]. The disclosure says phone calls may earn compensation [capture /advertising-disclosure/]. [inference] The partner numbers are probably tracked lines; who answers them is unknown.

## Trust, verification and reviews
- **Partner cards.** The evidence pack records Safeway Moving ("best overall value", 4.9/5), American Van Lines ("most experienced mover", 4.9/5) and North American Van Lines ("best customized plans") on every captured city, state and route page; some pages add other partner cards, and card ratings run from 4.2 to 4.9 [capture]. Safeway leads the interstate picks in the LA and Orlando meta descriptions [capture /movers/ca/los-angeles/long-distance/, /movers/fl/orlando/long-distance/]. /about-us/ says sponsored or partner content is labeled, but the captured card text carries no such label (the capture is text only) [capture]. Affiliate ties "may influence … why they're placed on our site" [capture /advertising-disclosure/]. v1 recorded a claim that no mover pays for ranking; it was not re-confirmed [v1 §2.2].
- **Non-movers and claims.** /movers/ca/ carries PODS and portable-storage cards, one offering a "10% discount", and geo pages have DIY sections [capture /movers/ca/, /movers/ri/, /movers/ca/los-angeles/long-distance/]. Card claims such as "Same-day estimates" and "80+ years' experience" carry no source or date [capture /movers/fl/orlando/long-distance/, /movers/ca/los-angeles/long-distance/].
- **Licensing.** USDOT numbers appear in body text for the three national picks, unchecked against FMCSA here [capture /movers/ca/long-distance/]. Cards elsewhere say only "licensed insured", though the method claims to confirm "broker vs. carrier status" [capture /movers/ca/los-angeles/long-distance/, /blog/binding-vs-non-binding-estimates/].
- **Badges and protection.** "moveBuddha Certified" and "Our Verified program" overlap, and the evidence does not show whether they are one program, how often they are rechecked or whether movers pay [capture /, /about-us/]. Certified movers bring "an extra $1,000 in move coverage and dedicated support", and the home page frames this as booking "through moveBuddha" [capture /movers/ri/, /]. The results page pairs "Up to $1,000 in added move protection" with the specialist call [walk /moving-cost-calculator-tool/]. [inference] Together these suggest a role beyond passing on leads; this is not a legal conclusion.
- **Method and scores.** Weights such as "Moving services (35%)" are published, but the method sits in several places and reads differently by list type [capture /blog/binding-vs-non-binding-estimates/, /movers/ri/, /, /about-us/]. Geo pages show a composite score as stars, built partly from "moveBuddha's own score" [capture /movers/ri/, /movers/ca/]. The disclosure calls its editorial reviews "personal opinions and experience", while geo pages cite an "analysis" [capture /advertising-disclosure/, /movers/ca/los-angeles/long-distance/]. [inference] The "verified reviews" counts appear to be reviews aggregated from 25+ outside sources. No reviews collected by moveBuddha itself were captured [capture]. Cards label Yelp and BBB ratings by source, and low scorers stay visible, such as a BBB "F", 66.2%-positive row [capture /movers/ri/, /movers/ca/].
- **Consumer guidance.** Guides teach an FMCSA check, the route page gives FMCSA's complaint line, and state pages explain consumer rights and released-value coverage [capture /blog/usdot-lookup/, /popular-routes/ca/fl/, /movers/ri/]. Membership and testimonial sections exist, but their names and text were not captured [capture /, /about-us/].
- **Scale.** The figure 400,000 describes people who trust its picks, people it connects to movers and tool users on three different pages, with no source [capture /movers/ca/los-angeles/long-distance/, /movers/ri/, /popular-routes/]. Reviews read "1,000,000+" on the home page and "4,000+" in state boilerplate [capture /, /movers/ca/long-distance/]. Organic visits come to roughly 460,000 a year, but visits are not people, so the claims can't be tested [semrush keyword-research §1].

## Disclosure and consent
- The "Advertising Disclosure" link opens every page's text and is the first element above the fold, on desktop and mobile, on the 6 pages where fold order was recorded [capture]. Its 294-word page is hedged and names no payer or pricing model [capture /advertising-disclosure/]. [inference] The payers are probably featured movers or intermediaries.
- The walk notes record no compensation statement on the results screen or in the partner form, and no consent screen was reached [walk].
- The footer carries a "Do Not Sell or Share" link, the standard state-privacy opt-out; the privacy policy and terms were not captured, so how webform data is shared is unknown [capture].
- The partner form runs on a separately named host, my.bestinterstatemoving.com, whose owner the evidence does not show [walk].
- Clarity and Hotjar (tools that offer session recording), Mixpanel, Facebook, Bing UET, Google Tag Manager and Ads, Reddit, an OpenAI pixel host and Google Maps load across the pooled 14-page scan, with no consent-certificate script; which page loads which tag, and whether recording was on, is unknown [capture].
- Guides embed a "Free Instant Quote" widget marked "No personal info required", whose output was not walked, while the calculator calls its output an estimate [capture /blog/usdot-lookup/, /moving-cost-calculator/]. /movers/ca/ pairs "Low demand" with "Get my best rate →" [capture /movers/ca/]. "Save up to 30% on your move" and "rates typically drop 15–20%" state no method [walk /moving-cost-calculator-tool/; capture /movers/ca/los-angeles/long-distance/]. v1 had recorded the savings claim as 30% or more [v1 §4].

## SEO and content
- **Drivers.** The calculator draws about 4,399 visits a month from 961 keywords; with the car shipping calculator (748), the tools bring 5,147 visits, about 13.4% and the largest family in the top 15 [semrush top_pages]. It ranks #4 for "moving cost calculator" (5,400 searches a month, keyword difficulty 49), where nine of the ten top-10 URLs contain "calculator" [semrush phrase_these, phrase_organic_top10].
- **Geo yield.** City long-distance pages earn 460–956 visits each, /movers/ri/ 222 and two state routes 133–156; about 64.5% of traffic comes from pages outside the top 15 [semrush top_pages].
- **Weak fit.** PODS and 1-800-GOT-JUNK cost guides, a countries-that-pay-you post and the car shipping calculator bring 3,664 visits, more than the three city long-distance pages (2,241) [semrush top_pages]. The USDOT guide gets 509 visits from 195 keywords, against 4,400 monthly searches for "usdot number lookup" [semrush top_pages, phrase_these]. One search run for that term was led by freight tools, with no moveBuddha URL near the top [search: https://www.dat.com/solutions/usdot-number-lookup, third party]. [inference] Freight users may drive much of that demand.
- **URLs.** Geo pages nest under /movers/{st}/ with 2-letter codes, routes under /popular-routes/{st}/{st}/ and many tools under /blog/; calculator results open at /moving-cost-calculator-tool/ [capture; semrush top_pages; walk]. v1 also recorded numeric-ID profiles, a city-to-city route and /movers/tx/buda/long-distance/ [v1 §2.2]. [inference] That suggests pages built from place lists rather than demand.
- **Templates.** LA and Orlando share 10 of 12 heading patterns and RI and CA 12 of 12, with paragraphs repeated word for word or lightly reworded; mainly the data differs [capture]. Link modules point to nearby cities and routes, and the footer lists hubs, not cities [capture /movers/ri/, /movers/ca/los-angeles/long-distance/].
- **Numbers.** California interstate costs read "$613 for a 1-bedroom to $2,622 for a 4-bedroom home" on the LA page but "between $990 and $4,033" on /movers/ca/long-distance/ [capture]. The route title ("$1,340-$8,110") and meta ("$4,000–$8,000") differ, though the page sources its average to "847 real-time quotes" over 90 days [capture /popular-routes/ca/fl/]. California mover counts vary by page because each counts a different group [capture /movers/ca/, /movers/ca/long-distance/]. The calculator cites "anonymized user quotes from this very tool" and says its inputs update both "continuously" and "monthly" [capture /moving-cost-calculator/].
- **Freshness.** Bylines, recorded on 5 of 14 pages, name Ryan Carrigan; /movers/ca/ shows the capture date as "Last Updated", the templated LA page claims "a decade" of personal experience, and "5 min read" covers 400- and 9,312-word pages [capture /movers/ca/, /movers/ca/los-angeles/long-distance/, /popular-routes/].
- **Citable shapes.** Guides open with a quick answer and compare USDOT and MC numbers, the LA page has a key-takeaways section, and the route page has a "Who regulates interstate moves?" box [capture /blog/usdot-lookup/, /movers/ca/los-angeles/long-distance/, /popular-routes/ca/fl/]. One search tool's summaries quoted moveBuddha picks and prices that differ from the live pages; these are not observed AI-engine citations [search: own pages, e.g. https://www.movebuddha.com/popular-routes/ca/fl/ca-to-orlando/].
- **Markup, titles and links.** Geo pages are typed Article, the calculator has a block that reads as FAQ, and complete type lists show no aggregateRating [capture]. Six of 14 titles add a bracketed promise, such as a year or price range [capture]. One search index holds ?p= and non-www versions of its pages [search: own pages, e.g. https://www.movebuddha.com/?p=1054991]. It says "Our data has been cited by The New York Times, Bloomberg, and USA Today" beside an "As Seen On" strip [capture /moving-cost-calculator/]. [inference] Data-led PR may earn some links, but Semrush does not show their sources.

## Design and UX
Every page's text starts with the header; on the 6 pages with recorded fold order, desktop runs disclosure link, "Get My Quote", logo, then topic buttons [capture]. The home hero pairs a four-field form with an "As Seen On" strip, and long pages embed a one-field widget [capture /, /blog/usdot-lookup/]. City pages open with a one-sentence answer naming top picks and counts [capture /movers/ca/los-angeles/long-distance/]. On mobile only about ten header-first elements were recorded, so where the hero form lands on a phone is unknown [capture].

## What AMC should adopt or adapt
- **Ungated estimator** [walk; semrush]. Better: live ranges with a line naming missing fields, AMC's own no-contact promise, the full-service range first, container or freight figures labeled as general information, inputs that prefill the survey, and one versioned model with an automated consistency test. Serves value before contact (site-architecture §5.3, §6.3 rule 5).
- **Header "How we make money" link** [capture]. Better: repeat the model beside every quote button and at consent in definite words (participating movers pay for the requests they buy), with one role statement everywhere. Serves trust pages (consent copy §1, §7; site-architecture §7.1).
- **Sourced ranges** [capture /popular-routes/ca/fl/]. Better: sample size, period, source and "data as of" on every range; prices from member rate cards or cited studies; title, meta and body read one record; consumer-data aggregates only with a consent basis and §7.5 thresholds; thin blocks omitted so the page waits at the gate. Serves the real-data rule (site-architecture §4.1 G4, §6.3, §7.5).
- **Details before contact** [walk]. Better: "not sure yet" answers and labeled stages; at contact, say that participating movers, up to the number the consumer picks, may receive the details and will be named before submit. Serves the survey and named-seller consent (consent copy §3–§5).
- **Public method and consumer guides** [capture]. Better: one versioned "How we verify movers" page and one ratings page that separates computed numbers from judgment; FMCSA-sourced check-a-mover, movers-vs-brokers, rights and valuation guides linked from profiles and routes. Serves trust pages (mover-verification §6, §7; compliance facts §4).
- **Answer-first geo pages** [capture]. Better: date and population in the answer sentence, picks computed per market, component columns (years active, review count, share positive, interstate authority), low scorers kept, a one-field survey start as the single main call to action, and AMC's mobile order. Serves SEO, AI visibility and value before contact (site-architecture §7.4, §7.7; visual-direction §12).
- **USDOT on every card** [capture /movers/ca/long-distance/]. Better: add MC, an authority-type label and an FMCSA link; enforce movers-only by FMCSA authority fields, never by name, rejecting dual carrier-and-broker records. Serves movers only (mover-verification §1, §3; compliance facts §1).

## What AMC should avoid
- **Partner cards as picks** [capture]: keep the Preferred badge and any "Sponsored" placement apart from rank, score and answer sentences; keep container and DIY brands out of mover rankings; show company claims only with evidence (mover-verification §4, §5; visual-direction §9.4; site-architecture §0 decisions 5, 7).
- **Drifting numbers** [walk; capture]: one record per figure, an automated check across title, meta and body, and every metric defined, sourced and dated; never call visitors people "connected" to movers (site-architecture §6.3, §7.1).
- **Broker-like extras** [capture; walk]: no coverage promises, booking or specialist-arranged bids; send any future guarantee to counsel. [inference] FMCSA's 2023 guidance weighs involvement in the transaction (compliance facts §2).
- **Quote, best-rate and savings wording** [capture; walk]: say "estimate range", name price drivers, note that a written estimate needs the mover's survey, and show seasonality only as request demand (compliance facts §2; site-architecture §6.3 rule 2).
- **Murky collection** [walk; capture]: street addresses first, an unclear "us", a persona with no stated employer, a separate form domain, or ad pixels and unmasked recording on survey and consent screens; keep a consent certificate or append-only ledger (compliance facts §3).
- **Imported "verified" reviews and stacked badges** [capture]: use AMC's evidence labels and one badge, and name memberships or testimonials only with proof and permission (mover-verification §4, §6, §7).
- **Absolute no-sale lines** [inference]: counsel should test consent copy §3's "We don't sell your details to anyone else" against state "sale" and "sharing" definitions.
- **Template filler** [capture; v1]: swap-test failures, small places that belong on the metro page, indexed city pairs, auto-bumped dates, first-person lines on templates, CMS ID URLs and FAQPage markup (site-architecture §4.1 G7–G8, §7.3, §8.5).

## Gaps and open questions
- Consent wording on any form; the partner form after email, the marketplace after screen 1, the bid path and the exit-intent box.
- How many movers a bid or marketplace request reaches, who sets the number, whether partner-form details go only to the named mover, and who employs the specialist.
- The privacy policy, terms, the operator of my.bestinterstatemoving.com and which tags run in the widget's later steps.
- Mover side: how movers join, what is paid, the pricing model, caps and refunds; the Certified program's mover terms appeared only in search and were not re-checked.
- The FMCSA authority behind USDOT 3166589 and the other partners, the $1,000 coverage terms, and any visual "sponsored" marker.
- No profile, local city, city-pair or thin-coverage page was captured; JSON-LD lists are clipped, and robots and canonical tags are not in the packs.
- Semrush keeps the top 15 pages only, with no keyword, trend or referring-domain detail; AI answer engines were not tested.
- Ownership, legal entity, BBB status and legal record were not re-checked; one capture day cannot show whether "Last Updated" dates reflect real edits.
