# moveBuddha: reference review

A moving research and review site, founded in 2015 by its own account, that pairs a no-contact cost calculator with partner quote forms, a "Mover Marketplace" and a specialist-call bid path [capture /moving-cost-calculator/, /movers/; walk]. It is on the list as the best model for an ungated estimator and data-led geo pages, and as a warning about partner cards inside rankings [inference].

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-27. **Evidence:** 14 of 14 pages captured; guided walks of the calculator (prices with no contact details), its results page, the marketplace's first screen and one partner form up to its email step, with nothing entered there; Semrush US, October 2026; single-run WebSearch results that the verifier could not re-run, so search-only claims about ownership, legal record and competitor programs are left out. **Confidence:** medium, because no consent wording, privacy policy, mover-facing page or post-email screen was seen.

## At a glance
- It calls itself "a moving research and review platform" [capture /moving-cost-calculator/] but runs lead paths [walk], and says it "may receive compensation" for clicks, webform submissions or calls [capture /advertising-disclosure/].
- The calculator showed three price ranges before any contact field [walk]. It is the top organic page: about 4,399 visits a month, 11.47% of the site [semrush top_pages].
- Numbers drift: the widget and its own results page priced the same move differently, with freight's top falling from $7,814 to $1,761 [walk], and route titles, metas and scale claims disagree [capture].
- The same national partner cards, with superlatives, ratings and phone numbers, recur on geo and route pages, with no "sponsored" label in the captured text [capture].
- The partner form asks nine move-detail screens before email; no consent wording was reached on any path [walk].
- About 38,335 US organic visits a month, Authority Score 44 and 9,427 referring domains [semrush domain_rank, backlinks_overview].

## Mover side
None of the 14 captured pages is aimed at movers. No join path, fees, sharing caps, exclusivity or lead checks were seen, and the footer has no mover sign-up link [capture]. Partner cards show toll-free numbers, local movers' cards show local ones, and Safeway's card number doubles as the form's "call us" line [capture /movers/ri/, /movers/ca/; walk]. [inference] These are probably tracked lines; who answers is unknown. AMC should publish lead prices, sharing caps and the credit policy on its public pricing page and in the portal (site-architecture §1.2; shared-pricing-and-credits), and settle its phone-number policy before profiles launch (site-architecture §0 decision 4).

## Trust, verification and reviews
- **Partner cards.** The evidence pack records Safeway Moving ("best overall value", 4.9/5), American Van Lines and North American Van Lines on every captured city, state and route page; other partner cards rate 4.2 to 4.9 [capture]. Safeway leads the interstate pick in the LA and Orlando metas, and the binding-estimates guide ends with partner cards [capture /movers/ca/los-angeles/long-distance/, /blog/binding-vs-non-binding-estimates/]. /about-us/ says partner content is labeled, but the text-only capture shows no label on cards [capture]. The disclosure says affiliate ties "may influence … why they're placed on our site" [capture /advertising-disclosure/].
- **Non-movers and claims.** /movers/ca/ carries a PODS card with a "10% discount", and cards make unsourced claims such as "Same-day estimates" [capture /movers/ca/, /movers/fl/orlando/long-distance/].
- **Licensing.** USDOT numbers appear in body text for three national picks on /movers/ca/long-distance/, while cards elsewhere say only "licensed insured", though the method claims to confirm "broker vs. carrier status" [capture /blog/binding-vs-non-binding-estimates/]. This review did not check them against FMCSA.
- **Badges and protection.** "moveBuddha Certified" (home) and "Our Verified program" (/about-us/) overlap; the evidence does not show whether they are one program, how often they recheck or whether movers pay [capture]. /movers/ri/ ties "an extra $1,000 in move coverage" to Certified movers, the home page frames booking "through moveBuddha", and results offer added protection [capture; walk]. [inference] This suggests a role beyond passing on leads; it is not a legal conclusion.
- **Scores.** Weights such as "Moving services (35%)" are published, but the method appears in several places and differs by list type [capture /blog/binding-vs-non-binding-estimates/, /movers/ri/]. Geo stars are a composite "moveBuddha score" that includes "moveBuddha's own score" [capture /movers/ri/, /movers/ca/]. The disclosure calls editorial reviews "personal opinions", while geo pages cite an "analysis" of thousands of reviews [capture /advertising-disclosure/, /movers/ca/los-angeles/long-distance/]. [inference] "Verified reviews" appear to be reviews aggregated from 25+ sources. Low scorers stay visible, such as a BBB "F" row [capture /movers/ca/].
- **Scale.** "400,000+" means people who trust, people connected and tool users on three pages, with no source or date [capture /movers/ca/los-angeles/long-distance/, /movers/ri/, /popular-routes/]. The home page claims 1,000,000+ reviews, state boilerplate "4,000+" [capture].

## Disclosure and consent
- The page text begins with the "Advertising Disclosure" link on all 14 pages; on the 6 pages with fold order recorded, it is first above the fold on desktop and mobile [capture]. The 294-word page names no payer or pricing model [capture /advertising-disclosure/]. [inference] Payers are probably featured movers or intermediaries.
- The walk notes record no compensation statement on the results screen or in the partner form [walk].
- A footer "Do Not Sell or Share" link is the standard state-privacy opt-out; the privacy policy and terms were not captured [capture].
- The partner form runs on a separately named host, my.bestinterstatemoving.com, whose owner is not shown [walk].
- Clarity and Hotjar (which offer session recording), Facebook, Bing, Google, Reddit, Mixpanel and an OpenAI pixel host load in the pooled 14-page scan; no consent-certificate script was detected [capture]. Which page loads which tag, and whether recording is on, is unknown.
- Wording blurs price and quote: a "Free Instant Quote" widget, whose output was not walked, sits beside calculator "estimates", and /movers/ca/ pairs "Low demand" with "Get my best rate →" [capture /blog/usdot-lookup/, /moving-cost-calculator/, /movers/ca/]. "Save up to 30% on your move" and "rates typically drop 15–20%" carry no method [walk; capture /movers/ca/los-angeles/long-distance/].

## SEO and content
- **Drivers.** The two calculators bring 5,147 visits (13.42%), and the site ranks #4 for "moving cost calculator" (5,400 searches a month, keyword difficulty 49) [semrush top_pages, phrase_these, phrase_organic_top10]. City long-distance pages earn 460–956 visits, /movers/ri/ 222 and two state routes 133–156; 64.5% of estimated traffic comes from pages outside the top 15 [semrush top_pages].
- **Weak fit.** Brand-cost (PODS, 1-800-GOT-JUNK), countries-that-pay and car-shipping pages bring 3,664 visits, more than the three city long-distance pages (2,241) [semrush top_pages]. The USDOT guide gets 509 visits against 4,400 monthly searches for "usdot number lookup", and freight tools led that query in one search tool [semrush; search: dat.com, third party]. [inference] Much of that demand may come from freight users.
- **URLs.** Geo pages nest under /movers/{st}/, routes under /popular-routes/{st}/{st}/ and many tools under /blog/ [capture; semrush]. v1 also recorded numeric-ID profiles, a city-to-city route and a small-place page (/movers/tx/buda/long-distance/) [v1 §2.2].
- **Templates.** LA and Orlando share 10 of 12 heading patterns, RI and CA 12 of 12, and several paragraphs repeat or are lightly reworded; the unique parts are data [capture].
- **Numbers.** California interstate costs read "$613 for a 1-bedroom to $2,622" on the LA page and "between $990 and $4,033" on /movers/ca/long-distance/ [capture]. The route title says "$1,340-$8,110", its meta "$4,000–$8,000" [capture /popular-routes/ca/fl/]. The counterexample is the same page's "847 real-time quotes provided to moveBuddha users over the past 90 days" [capture /popular-routes/ca/fl/].
- **Freshness.** Bylines on 5 of 14 pages name the founder; /movers/ca/ shows the capture date as "Last Updated"; the templated LA page claims a decade of first-person experience; "5 min read" appears on 400- and 9,312-word pages [capture].
- **Citable shapes.** Guides open with a quick answer, teach an FMCSA check and compare USDOT and MC numbers, and the route page has a "Who regulates interstate moves?" box [capture /blog/usdot-lookup/, /popular-routes/ca/fl/]. One search tool's summaries quoted moveBuddha figures that differ from the live pages; these are not observed AI-engine citations [search: movebuddha.com's own pages].
- **Markup and titles.** Geo pages use Article, the calculator has a block that reads as FAQ, and complete type lists show no aggregateRating [capture]. Six of 14 titles add a bracketed promise [capture].

## Design and UX
Where fold order was recorded (6 pages), the desktop header runs disclosure link, "Get My Quote", logo, then topic buttons [capture]. The home hero pairs a four-field form with "As Seen On", and long pages embed a one-field quote widget [capture /, /blog/usdot-lookup/]. City pages open with the answer and counts, then carry nearby-city and routes-from modules; the footer lists service hubs, not city links [capture /movers/ca/los-angeles/long-distance/, /movers/ri/]. Where the hero form lands on a phone is not shown [capture].

## What AMC should adopt or adapt
- **Ungated estimator.** Lead with the full-service range, label container or freight figures as general information, prefill the survey, and feed estimator, results and cost blocks from one versioned model with a consistency test. Serves: value before contact (site-architecture §5.3, §6.3 rule 5).
- **Header "how we make money" link.** Also state the model beside quote buttons and at consent, in definite words: movers pay for the requests they buy. Serves: trust pages (consent copy §1, §7).
- **Sourced ranges.** Show sample size, period, source and "data as of"; take price ranges from member rate cards or cited studies, and omit thin blocks. Serves: real-data SEO rule (site-architecture §4.1, §6.3 rules 2 and 4).
- **Details before contact.** Keep "not sure yet" answers and stages; the contact screen says participating movers, up to the consumer's chosen number, may receive the details and are named before submit. Serves: quote survey (consent copy §3–§5).
- **Public method and mover-check guides.** One versioned verification page, one ratings page separating computed numbers from judgment, and check-a-mover, movers-vs-brokers and rights guides from FMCSA sources (state pages carry a rights section [capture /movers/ri/]). Serves: trust pages (mover-verification §6, §7; site-architecture §3.1).
- **Answer-first geo pages.** Put the date and population in the answer sentence, show component columns and keep low scorers; keep AMC's mobile order (H1, lede, survey step 1). Serves: SEO and AI visibility (site-architecture §7.4, §7.7; visual-direction §12).
- **USDOT on every card.** Add MC, an authority-type label and an FMCSA link, and enforce movers-only by FMCSA authority fields, not names. Serves: movers only (mover-verification §1, §3; compliance facts §1).

## What AMC should avoid
- **Partner cards as picks.** Keep the Preferred badge and any "Sponsored" slot apart from rank and score, and keep non-movers out of rankings (visual-direction §9.4; site-architecture §0 decisions 5 and 7; mover-verification §4).
- **Drifting numbers.** One record feeds title, meta, body and tools, CI fails mismatches, and every metric is defined, sourced and dated (site-architecture §6.3; compliance facts §4 for review counts).
- **Broker-like extras.** No coverage promises, booking or specialist bids; [inference] FMCSA's 2023 guidance weighs involvement in the transaction (compliance facts §2; consent copy §1).
- **Quote and savings language.** Say "estimate range", name price drivers, require the mover's survey for a written estimate, and make no savings claims (compliance facts §2; mover-verification §5).
- **Murky collection.** No street addresses first, unclear "us", persona without an employer or separate form domain; no unmasked recording or ad pixels on survey and consent screens; keep an append-only consent ledger (site-architecture §7.1; compliance facts §3).
- **"Verified" imported reviews and stacked badges.** Use AMC's evidence labels and one badge (mover-verification §4, §6; compliance facts §4).
- **Absolute no-sale lines.** Counsel should test consent copy §3's "We don't sell your details to anyone else" against state "sale" definitions.
- **Template filler and permutations.** Swap-test failures, city pairs, small-place clones, fake freshness and FAQPage markup (site-architecture §4.1, §7.3, §8.5; compliance facts §5).

- How many movers a bid or marketplace request reaches, who sets that number, and who employs the specialist.
- The privacy policy, the terms and the operator of my.bestinterstatemoving.com.
- Mover side: join path, what is paid, pricing model, caps and refunds.
- The FMCSA authority behind USDOT 3166589 and the other partners, the $1,000 coverage terms and any visual "sponsored" marker.
- No profile, local city, city-pair or thin-coverage page was captured; JSON-LD lists are clipped; Semrush covers the top 15 pages only; AI answer engines were not tested.
