# HomeGuide (moving companies): reference review

A pay-per-lead directory for many home-service trades whose moving hub the owner listed; it matters to AMC for its lead model and cost guides, not as a moving SEO rival [search: /pro; v1 §2.6; semrush].

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-27. **Evidence:** capture blocked (Cloudflare challenge, HTTP 403); no walks; Semrush US, October 2026; WebSearch; v1 §2.6. Search paths are HomeGuide's own pages, and `help/N` is help.homeguide.com/article/N. **Confidence:** low, because no page or flow was observed, so everything outside Semrush rests on search excerpts, some not re-run.

## At a glance
- Pros pay per lead and consumers pay nothing; no consumer-facing note that pros pay was found [search: /pro, help/306].
- The Terms name "Liaison" as provider, and the same terms run four other marketplaces [search: /terms-of-service].
- A request's phone number goes to at most five pros, a cap HomeGuide sets [search: help/83].
- Text consent sits in the Terms and covers unnamed partners; the form was not seen [search: /terms-of-service; capture].
- "Every pro on HomeGuide is reviewed", with no explanation of that review [search: /safety].
- Measured moving pages earn about 0.4% of the domain's 326,101 monthly visits [semrush].
- The cost-guide format is worth adapting, though the guides earn little traffic [search; semrush].

## Consumer journey
| step | what it asks | what it shows before contact | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Hub or city page | Free quotes | A "10 Best" list; city price ranges and tips | [search: /moving-companies title; v1 §2.6] |
| 2. Request | About a dozen questions; phone recommended | Not observed | [search: /pro/how-it-works, help/83] |
| 2b. Direct pick | Picking one pro (a Direct Lead) | The list | [search: /pro] |
| 3. Matching | Nothing | Not observed | [search: help/306] |
| 4. Follow-up | Nothing | After contact: up to five pros get the phone number; others may message | [search: help/83, help/306] |

- The cap covers the phone number only, no consumer choice was found, and the source is a mid-2023 help article [search: help/83; inference].
- /pro says every lead has a phone number, while the consumer article only recommends one [search: /pro, help/83].
- A dozen questions is close to AMC's eight input steps (lead-lifecycle §2) [inference]. Whether the flow shows any price or preview is unknown [capture].

## Mover side
Profiles are free with no subscription, and lead prices vary with competition and job size but are not published [search: /pro, help/306]. Exact matches are billed automatically within a weekly budget, other leads on acceptance, and messages sent from the Opportunities page only on a reply [search: help/306, help/486]. A pro out of budget leaves search results until Monday [search: help/486]. Pros can report a lead for a refund when the customer first answers with a clear no [search: help/306]. Pro software is sold as "#1 FREE" (/for-business), but its terms describe a paid "Messages Plus" texting tier [search: /terms-of-service/terms-pos]. A few pro reviews dispute lead quality; the sample is small [search: ca.trustpilot.com, third party].

## Trust, verification and reviews
- **Vetting.** /safety explains its badges but not the blanket review claim, and "reviewed" blurs vetting with customer reviews [search: /safety; inference].
- **Badges.** A license badge (found in a public database), a background-check badge (may not cover your worker) and Best of (top 10% yearly on positive reviews); paid status is not stated [search: /safety]. One mover's business name includes the word Verified [search: a /fl/delray-beach/ listing].
- **Credentials.** No profile was seen, and no structured USDOT or MC field, check date or carrier-or-broker label appeared anywhere [capture; search].
- **Reviews.** Pros are coached to ask their own customers, and reviewer checks are not explained [search: help center; /reviews]. With Best of built on positive reviews, that rewards asking only happy customers [inference].
- **Lists.** Many listings rest on 0 to 5 reviews or one 5.0, beside mover-written claims such as a guaranteed quote [v1 §2.6; search: /co/denver/local-movers/, /fl/miami/local-movers/, not re-run]. No ranking method was found [search].
- **Placement.** Pros out of budget drop from search results, so budget partly shapes what consumers see [search: help/486; inference].
- **Record.** No FTC, attorney general, BBB or court record for HomeGuide turned up, which proves nothing [search]. The FTC's 2022 complaint against HomeAdvisor, another pay-per-lead platform (Docket No. 9407), alleged misrepresented leads, unsupported conversion claims and an add-on month misrepresented as free; the consent order (up to $7.2 million) became final in April 2023 [search: ftc.gov, regulator].

## Disclosure and consent
- **Who pays.** Consumer pages call the service free, while lead fees are explained on pro pages and in the help center [search: /moving-companies, /pro, help/306].
- **Operator.** /terms-of-service and the software terms name Liaison, and identical terms appear on lessons.com, tutors.com, vety.com and trustedcare.com, suggesting one multi-brand operator [search: own and sibling sites; inference]. One third-party profile places HomeGuide in Austin, Texas (not re-confirmed) [search: cbinsights.com].
- **Texts.** Using the service counts as consent to automated, possibly promotional texts from Liaison and its unnamed "Partners"; an SMS section adds opt-in wording, a not-required-to-buy line and STOP, HELP and START [search: /terms-of-service]. An older help article names HomeGuide as sender [search: help/277]. No consent language for automated or prerecorded calls was found [search].
- **Privacy and disputes.** The privacy policy says SMS opt-in data is not shared for others' marketing, which sits oddly with partner consent; it also covers call recording [search: /privacy-policy; inference]. No arbitration clause appeared in the Terms excerpts [search: /terms-of-service].
- **Booking.** The Terms call HomeGuide a booking platform, and consumer pages invite people to book pros [search: /terms-of-service, /moving-companies].

## SEO and content
- **Domain vs moving.** HomeGuide has 512,002 keywords and $2.49M traffic value, the highest of the 11 references [semrush: domain_rank]. Measured moving URLs earn about 1,239 visits a month, a floor, as only URLs containing "movers" or "moving" were pulled, 25 rows each [semrush: top_pages_more]. So the discovery brief §3 and keyword-research §1 overstate its moving weight, and keyword-research §5 wrongly says these pages were not pulled [inference].
- **Owner's page.** /moving-companies has 2,056 keywords but 129 visits, which suggests low positions for near-me queries [semrush; inference].
- **URLs.** Root hubs (/local-movers), city × service pages under two-letter states (/tx/dallas/local-movers/), nested profiles and /costs/ guides; no state hub, route page or calculator appeared [semrush; v1 §5].
- **Templates.** Five or more services repeat by city; apart from Rancho Cucamonga (464 visits), measured city × service pages earn 36 or fewer, and modifier hubs sharing one title template earn little (/apartment-movers 8) [semrush; search].
- **SERPs.** HomeGuide is absent from all 14 saved top-10 SERPs [semrush: phrase_organic_top10]. For "how much do movers cost" (9,900/mo, KD 32), four of the top 10 are cost pages for one place, yet national guides from extraspace.com and consumeraffairs.com also rank [semrush].
- **Cost guides.** Titles are questions with a year, and ranges give a unit, a minimum and a season [search: /costs/moving-company-costs; v1 §2.6]. That page earns 10 visits, against v1's "strongest pattern" (§2.6) [semrush]. One non-moving guide credits editor and fact-checker to "Editorial staff", and cost claims state no sample or method [search: /costs/electrical-work-pricing-guide].
- **Numbers and dates.** One long-distance range spans about 13 times [search]. City titles such as /fl/deerfield-beach/local-movers/ carry "(2026)", and whether years match updates is unknown [search].
- **Presence.** Moving queries naming HomeGuide returned other sites, on one engine on one day [search].

## Design and UX
The browser got a 37-word challenge page, "Just a moment..." (HTTP 403), so layout, calls to action, form and JSON-LD are unknown [capture: /moving-companies]. v1 reported a profile stat row (rating, reviews, hires, employees, years in business); 2026 search summaries re-show years in business, with no stated source [v1 §2.6; search]. Google evidently crawls the site; AI crawlers are unknown [semrush; inference].

## What AMC should adopt or adapt
- **Published cap** [search: help/83]. Better: the consumer picks 1 to N (5 proposed, as at HomeGuide), the movers are named at consent, and the count caps every channel. Serves the sharing-limit question and named consent (consent draft §4–§6; shared-pricing §9; lead-lifecycle §3 rule 4).
- **Direct pick** [search: /pro]. Better: the profile quote button still runs the full survey and named consent, and is exclusive only if the consumer chooses 1. Serves exclusive-lead rules (mover-verification §5; lead-lifecycle §2).
- **Value before contact** [search]. Better: a labeled cost range (not an estimate; counsel words it) and a matched-mover preview before contact details, moving part of step 8 ahead of step 6. Serves value before contact (lead-lifecycle §2; compliance facts §2).
- **Cost-guide shape** [search]. Better: a question H1 and a scoped range with unit, minimum and assumptions, from §6.3 rule 2 sources with sample, period and method, fed by one dataset. Serves /moving-costs/ and the G4 block (site-architecture §4.1, §6.3, §7.4).
- **Badges and stat rows** [search: /safety; v1 §2.6]. Better: covers and does-not-cover notes, verified-on dates, sourced stats (FMCSA authority, labeled review counts), badges unlike company names, and no review-score badge or lead counts that read as jobs. Serves Preferred Movers and profiles (consent draft §8; mover-verification §4, §5).
- **Open lead rules** [search: help/306, help/486]. Better: publish price bands, the share cap, credits and claim reasons (a consumer declining is not one) before sign-up, keep preview-then-buy, and make any auto-buy opt-in with a weekly cap. Serves the marketplace (shared-pricing §1–§6; lead-lifecycle §5).
- **Portal CRM** [search: /terms-of-service/terms-pos]. Better: separate terms, messages only within each consumer's consent, no bulk marketing to bought leads, and plain free-tier charges. Serves the company portal (consent draft §11; compliance facts §3).
- **Bot rules** [capture]. Better: challenge at quote submission, allowlist verified crawlers, and confirm with Search Console, Bing and real crawler hits in logs, not user-agent tests. Serves SEO and AI visibility (site-architecture §7.6; compliance facts §6).
- **Smaller markets** [semrush]. Better: score suburbs inside Wave 1 states; directional, from one outlier. Serves rollout (site-architecture §5.3).

## What AMC should avoid
- Saying who pays only on pro pages; say it at quote buttons, in the survey and at consent (consent draft §1, §7).
- Terms-buried consent for a parent brand and unnamed partners. AMC's unticked box names each mover and USDOT number, is versioned and hashed, and matches the SMS sender name; PEWC fit is for counsel (consent draft §5; compliance facts §3; site-architecture §7.1).
- Unexplained vetting and free-text credentials; publish each check and source, key on USDOT, require MC while require_mc_number is on, and exclude brokers (mover-verification §1, §3, §7; compliance facts §1).
- Pro-chosen review invitations; AMC sends every invitation and monitors those movers request (mover-verification §6; compliance facts §4).
- Best, near-me or fixed-count list titles, and one-review ratings that outrank; publish the ordering rule (site-architecture §4.6, §8.2).
- Visibility tied to lead budget; label any paid slot Sponsored (mover-verification §4; consent draft §7).
- The city × service grid beyond the gated city long-distance pages; below-gate pages stay unpublished (site-architecture §4.2; compliance facts §5).
- Booking language or handling move money; counsel should review any portal invoicing (compliance facts §2).
- Unprovable lead-quality claims and "free" add-ons that later charge, both HomeAdvisor counts (lead-lifecycle §4; shared-pricing §6) [search: ftc.gov].
- Year stamps without a data refresh (site-architecture §7.3), and benchmarking on HomeGuide's domain totals.

## Gaps and open questions
- The live request flow, the contact step and any preview before contact.
- Form consent wording, certificate scripts and opt-out handling.

- How lists are ordered and what HomeGuide's review of pros involves.
- Reviewer verification and the reviews-policy text.
- What the license badge checks for movers, and broker screening.
- Lead prices, refund rules, the full Terms and Privacy text, and Liaison's legal entity.
