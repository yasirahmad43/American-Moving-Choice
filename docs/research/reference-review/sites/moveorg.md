# Move.org: reference review

Clearlink's editorial moving-review site, which says it earns from clicks, calls and sponsored placements; it is on the list as the publisher that ranks beside movers for AMC's core terms [capture /disclosure/; search: craft.co, third party; semrush phrase_organic_top10].

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-27. **Evidence:** 14 of 14 pages captured; automated walks of `/` and `/movers/` found no quote form; the Find-a-Mover tool was not walked; TrustedForm script detected; Semrush US (October 2026); standard WebSearch. **Confidence:** medium, because the quote tool and its live consent text were not observed.

## At a glance
- Move.org says it may earn money when readers click or call, runs sponsored placements, and pitches "qualified, ready-to-book leads" to partners [capture /disclosure/, /partnership/].
- Its owner, Clear Link Technologies, LLC (Utah), is a Foundever subsidiary per an October 2025 press release [search: businesswire.com press release; craft.co, third party].
- No captured main page has a quote form; conversion runs through a Find-a-Mover widget, phone numbers and brand cards [capture].
- About 107,657 estimated organic visits a month, mostly to adjacent-service and reference pages, and an Authority Score of 48 [semrush domain_rank, top_pages, backlinks].
- Trust signals look strong but lack method, and Move.org's own review calls its #1 long-distance pick a broker-carrier "Hybrid" [capture /disclosure/, /allied-moving-reviews/].
- The only reference so far with a consent-certificate script (TrustedForm) [capture].

Joining appears to run through sales: the team reviews each inquiry "across both editorial and partner strategy", and rankings and awards are listed under "Editorial opportunities" [capture /partnership/]. No prices, sharing caps, exclusivity, lead checks or refund terms appeared in the captured text [capture /partnership/].

## Trust, verification and reviews
- **Vetting.** /disclosure/ says it checks "federal standing" in the FMCSA database and uses "a 5-point rating criteria" [capture /disclosure/]. Its "we've vetted" and "verified movers" claims are never defined or dated in the captured text [capture /, /movers/texas/houston/].
- **License box.** The one review captured puts MC, USDOT, a "Hybrid" label, GPS tracking and safety rating under one FMCSA credit line, with no date captured [capture /allied-moving-reviews/]. GPS tracking is not FMCSA data [inference].
- **Broker vs carrier.** The long-distance list says every pick "holds its own FMCSA carrier authority" and "none are brokers reselling your job to a company you've never heard of" [capture /best-interstate-moving-companies/]. Yet the #1 pick's review says "Allied is a broker and a carrier" [capture /allied-moving-reviews/].
- **Paid placement.** "Preferred Partner" and "Featured Partner" spots are sponsored [capture /disclosure/]. A "Featured partner" heads the unnumbered "Other cross country moving companies we considered" section, and ranking cards carry "up to" discounts and phone numbers [capture /best-interstate-moving-companies/].
- **Independence.** The disclosure says affiliate ties "may influence" what is reviewed and where, but not the analysis [capture /disclosure/]. The #1 pick also won the 2026 long-distance award [search: move.org/best-movers-awards/, site's own page].
- **Ratings.** Houston mixes a "Customer rating" table (no source captured), a "Move.org rating" table, and a "5" on the lead tool's own card [capture /movers/texas/houston/]. Allied's card shows 4.9 with no basis, though the list says Allied "tends to score lower than some competitors" [capture /allied-moving-reviews/, /best-interstate-moving-companies/]. The capture shows no review text, company replies, Review markup, or source for the "moving companies evaluated 156" counter [capture /best-interstate-moving-companies/].

## Disclosure and consent
- On all six pages where order was recorded, the first item above the fold is "We may earn money when you click on our links. Read full disclosure.", with a close icon [capture /, /movers/]. /disclosure/ adds calls and paid placements, but its condensed captured text never mentions leads or says Move.org is not a mover or broker [capture /disclosure/].
- On Move.org's test page, the consent lets Clearlink (dba Move.org) and partners call and text, even at Do-Not-Call numbers, and says consent is not a condition of purchase [search: move.org/generic-form-test/, site's own test page]. An earlier excerpt tying mandatory arbitration to the submit button was not re-confirmed [search, same page]; v1 saw similar wording on /quote-allied/ [v1 §2.11].
- TrustedForm, HubSpot forms, CrazyEgg and Clarity scripts are present, though that does not prove they run [capture]. Ad scripts (Taboola, LinkedIn, Quora, Bing UET, Google Ads) sit beside a OneTrust notice saying partners may "build a profile of your interests"; whether any wait for consent is unknown [capture /].
- A 2020 TCPA class action, Fabrikant v. Clear Link Technologies (D. Neb. No. 8:20-cv-00470), alleges prerecorded calls without written consent; its outcome is unknown, and Move.org is not shown to be involved [search: docketalarm.com, third party]. Searches found no FTC or attorney general action and no Move.org BBB profile [search, no matching records].

## Design and UX
The mobile homepage shows the money line, a short H1, one CTA and a "Move.org is featured on:" press strip [capture /]. Article and city pages show no CTA among the ten elements the pack records [capture]. Bylines name authors, reviewers and "Data Analysis" credits [capture /best-interstate-moving-companies/]. No reviewer credentials were captured, and one reviewer also writes for the site [capture /moving-companies-military/, /things-to-know-about-moving-containers/].

## What AMC should adopt or adapt
- **Money line.** Say movers pay for the requests they buy, link a "How AMC works" hub, and repeat it at consent, where it can't be closed [inference]. Serves the trust pages (consent draft §7; lead-lifecycle §6).
- **License box.** Give each field a source and a verified-on date, add an FMCSA link, and keep marketing fields apart. Serves the USDOT/MC rule (mover-verification §5).
- **Honest cost limits.** Show a labeled range before any contact step and prefill the survey with it; counsel decides on ranges. Serves value before contact (compliance facts §2).
- **One-form promise.** Make it true: state the maximum, ask "how many companies", and name the movers at consent. Serves the survey and consent rules.
- **Consent certificate.** Use one only as a backup to AMC's own consent record. Keep replay tools off or masked on contact and consent steps (counsel). Serves consent evidence (compliance facts §3).
- **Labeled rating tables.** Show type, evidence and count on every rating, one value everywhere, and no rating on AMC's own tool. Serves reviews (mover-verification §6).
- **Answer-first guides.** Move.org's guides open with summaries, jump links and question headings [capture /how-to-perform-a-home-inventory/]. Add real credits, an honest AI statement, 49 CFR 375 sources and a regulatory definition of long distance. Serves SEO and AI visibility (site-architecture §7.2, §7.4).
- **Broker-vs-carrier explainer.** Target "movers vs brokers" (110/mo, KD 6) [semrush keyword-to-page-map.csv]. Say at consent that no broker receives requests. Serves movers-only (consent draft §2).
- **Separate partner page.** Make AMC's version self-serve, with pricing rules but not private prices, and no editorial offers. Serves the company portal (shared-pricing §4).

## What AMC should avoid
- Disclosures that cover clicks and calls but not the consumer's own request (consent draft §1, §7).
- Arbitration in the consent line, and consent for unnamed "partners" (consent draft §5).
- Calculator titles that gate a lead form, and "best-fit mover" or "book" wording (homepage meta [capture /]) that suggests AMC picks the mover, a broker risk (compliance facts §2).
- Paid labels in rankings, rankings or awards on mover-facing sales pages, and "independent" claims while movers pay AMC (compliance facts §4). "Preferred" already signals paid or affiliated status in this market [capture /disclosure/; CMR evidence pack]. Explain AMC's badge everywhere, and check the trademark.
- "Up to" savings claims, and mover phone numbers in rankings, since a direct call skips named-mover consent [inference] (input to site-architecture §0 item 4).
- "No brokers" claims, unless every listed company passed the authority check (mover-verification §1 item 3).
- Unsourced counters, relative dates and facts that conflict across pages. Keep one dated facts registry (site-architecture §6.2).
- City pages that open with reused national or sponsored blocks, the doorway pattern (compliance facts §5).
- Ad and replay scripts on the survey, contact and consent steps [inference]. Ask counsel whether selling requests counts as a "sale" (consent draft §3).
- Header links to single movers, as Move.org has for its top five [capture /], and near-duplicate best-of URLs (site-architecture §2.5).

## Gaps and open questions
- Find-a-Mover tool: steps, any preview before contact, the contact step, recipient count and confirmation.
- Live consent format, seller list and arbitration, plus the privacy policy and terms.
- Follow-up calls, and partner economics, including whether ranked or award-winning companies pay.
- Whether scripts fire inside the tool or before cookie consent.
- Owner decisions: is van-line eligibility judged per brand or per agent's USDOT [inference]? Do related brands count as one company toward the consumer's number? Move.org asks this of Allied and North American [capture /allied-moving-reviews/].
- Thin samples: one city page, 15 of 30 Semrush rows [semrush top_pages], six single search runs.
- Not re-confirmed from v1: /quote-allied/, mystery shopping and the HireAHelper partnership [v1 §2.11]. "Move, Inc." lawsuits concern Realtor.com's owner, not Move.org [search, third-party results].
