# Unpakt: reference review

An online moving marketplace where consumers book and pay through Unpakt [capture /how-it-works/, /]; it is the clearest example on the owner's list of the book-and-pay model AMC has ruled out [inference].

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-27. **Evidence:** 9 of 9 pages captured (www.unpakt.com; "blog" marks blog.unpakt.com); automated and guided walks of the home form, both stopped after the first screen; Semrush US; limited, unverified WebSearch. **Confidence:** medium: nothing past the first form screen, and no profile or city page, was seen.

## At a glance
- Unpakt presents itself as a place to book and pay: movers set a "cost per unit of stuff", a formula sets the price, and Unpakt pays the mover afterward [capture /how-it-works/, /].
- All six captured www pages mark Unpakt up as a MovingCompany rated 4.2 from 36 ratings; no visible rating was recorded [capture].
- The first screen presets move size and date; the guided walk then failed twice and landed on /sign-in/ [walk /].
- No captured page says how Unpakt itself is paid or how it checks movers, and none shows a USDOT or MC number [capture].
- About 5,976 estimated organic visits a month, 85% on two URLs, and no top-10 ranking for the 14 AMC target terms Semrush pulled [semrush domain_rank, top_pages_more, phrase_organic_top10].

- The guided walk submitted the defaults and the site tried to create a move plan, so unchosen values are accepted [walk /].
- The pitch is "No Phone Calls. Get Real Prices Now.", yet no captured page says whether, or how many, movers get contact details [capture /].
- The redirect suggests an account is needed; the failure may be sandbox-only [inference].

## Mover side
Partners, including real estate agents, earn a fee per booked move, paid monthly, and may offer clients "an exclusive discount for any moving company" (excerpt cut off) [capture /partnership/, footer]. A 13% mover fee (2012 press) and help-center commission tiers were not re-verified [search: betakit.com, third party; unpakt-mover-faq.groovehq.com, site's own].

## Trust, verification and reviews
- **Vetting.** Movers are "pre screened" (/partnership/) and "licensed" (/faq/), but no captured page gives a method or any USDOT, DOT or MC text [capture]. v1 reported license, insurance, registration and BBB checks [v1 §2.7]; a help-center screening page was not re-verified [search: unpakt-faqs.groovehq.com, site's own].
- **Guarantee.** The home page offers a "100% price and service guarantee", says movers "are obligated to honor your guaranteed price" and says quotes "are not based on estimates" [capture /]. The price comes instantly from the online inventory, before anyone sees the goods; whether movers survey later was not observed [capture /how-it-works/; walk].
- **Claims.** "800,000+ moves booked" and "save 45% on your moving costs on average" have no source or date [capture /]. "The only place" and "the the biggest online resource" are unsupported [capture /partnership/, blog / meta].
- **Ratings.** The 4.2 / 36 markup describes Unpakt itself, even on /sign-in/ [capture], and sits oddly beside 800,000+ bookings [inference]. Off-site scores were not re-verified, so none is repeated [search: uk.trustpilot.com, consumersadvocate.org, third party].
- **Reviews.** "Real customer reviews" are promised, but no captured page explains how they are collected or checked [capture / meta]. v1 said some profile reviews come from "other review portals" [v1 §2.7].

## Disclosure and consent
- **Role.** The home page says "our movers" yet presents a booking platform [capture /]. No captured page says whether Unpakt is a mover, broker or neither [capture]. Holding payments sits uneasily with v1's report that the Agreement disclaims "involvement or control over the actual transaction" [inference; v1 §2.7].
- **Payment.** "You don't pay the movers directly" [capture /]. AMC's mvp-scope §5 deposits-and-payments add-on candidate would put AMC in the money flow that 2023 FMCSA guidance weighs (compliance facts §2) [inference].
- **Consent.** No consent wording or consent-certificate script was seen, but the walk never reached the contact step where they would appear [walk; capture]. An "I want to connect utilities" option sits by the main button, its type and default unrecorded [capture /].
- **Tags.** Google Ads, Tag Manager, Facebook, Zoho PageSense and Cloudflare tags load; no cookie-consent host appears, though one could be self-hosted [capture].
- **Privacy.** The policy of "Unpakt, LLC" defines "we" to include its "affiliates", calls "item or shipment values" non-personal, and may draw data from "commercially available sources" and ad networks; the excerpts do not say what movers receive [capture /privacy/].

## SEO and content
- **Footprint.** 5,976 estimated visits, valued (ad-equivalent) at about $22.94 each, about twice the next reference site; the home page takes 65.72% and /moving-companies/NY/new-york/ 19.29% [semrush domain_rank, top_pages_more]. Unpakt is no search rival on AMC's Wave 1 terms [semrush phrase_organic_top10].
- **City pages.** Only New York earns real traffic (1,153 visits); five other city pages earn 9 to 48, even at KD 37 (Miami) and KD 30 (Columbus), and profiles and service pages 9 to 73 [semrush top_pages_more, phrase_these]. NYC has about 4 times Miami's demand but 24 times its visits [semrush top_pages_more; site-architecture §5.2].
- **URLs.** Uppercase state codes mix with lowercase cities (/moving-companies/FL/miami/), and profiles sit at /movers/{company}-{city}-{st}/ [semrush]. This corrects v1, which saw no geo URLs on the main domain [v1 §2.7].
- **Two hosts.** www (MovingCompany markup) and the blog (Organization) use different navigation [capture]. NYC intent overlaps: the www city page and a blog "Best Moving Companies in New York City" post [capture blog /; semrush].
- **Blog.** Seven blog URLs earn 473 visits, about 330 on population, museum, restaurant and home-type lists [semrush]. Dates are relative ("2 years ago" to "4 years ago"), one title says "in 2023", and the captured cost listings show no method or source [capture blog].
- **Markup.** /faq/ is 212 words of "READ MORE" links; no captured page shows an author or methodology page, or has FAQPage, BreadcrumbList, Article or Person markup [capture].
- **Hygiene.** /sign-in/ copies the home title and meta; the blog H1 is an image; four typos appear [capture].

## Design and UX
- On mobile, "The Moving App" link comes before the H1 on four www pages; the home "Moving From" field and a support link sit above the fold [capture /, /partnership/, /how-it-works/, /sign-in/].
- On desktop, promo, provider and login links precede the H1; the form's position was not recorded [capture /].

## What AMC should adopt or adapt
- **Move details first, from any page** [walk /; capture /how-it-works/]. Better: route fields after the H1 and lede on mobile and every explainer; no presets; a required size with bedrooms and square feet; a flexible-date option; stops and storage asked early. Serves lead-lifecycle §2, shared-pricing §6, visual-direction.md.
- **Price explainer** [capture /how-it-works/]. Better: a plain formula of the estimator's inputs on a versioned method page, shown as a range, plus steps stating the exclusive and shared rule. Serves site-architecture §6.3 rule 5.
- **Control instead of a no-calls promise** [capture /]. Better: the consumer picks how many movers and sees their names and verification dates first. Serves lead-lifecycle §2 steps 7-8.
- **City cost content** [capture blog /category/moving-cost/]. Better: ranges only from mover rate cards or cited studies, on the page they describe. Serves site-architecture §6.3 rule 2, §7.5.
- **A promise of real reviews** [capture / meta]. Better: label each review's evidence (inquiry, customer, completed move), never pass off imported reviews as AMC's, and publish a review methodology. Serves mover-verification §6; compliance facts §4.
- **Joining rules for the owner** [inference]. Options: own trucks, which help screen out brokers, and a minimum operating history. Serves mover-verification §1, §3.
- **Partner channel, later** [capture /partnership/]. Better: the same survey, named-mover consent and sharing limit; disclosure is a counsel question. Serves lead-lifecycle §2; compliance facts §4.
- **Reassurance as education** [capture /]. Better: estimate types, the 110% rule, safe payment and FMCSA links. Serves compliance facts §1-§2.

## What AMC should avoid
- Pricing, booking or taking payment for moves, discounting or guaranteeing movers' prices, and copy promising "no hidden fees" or denying that quotes are estimates (discovery brief §1; compliance facts §2) [capture /, /partnership/]; flag the mvp-scope §5 payments add-on for removal or counsel sign-off.
- Mover-type markup on AMC, "our movers" and self-ratings; use one Organization @id and a CI schema lint (site-architecture §7.1, §8.5, §9; compliance facts §2, §5) [capture].
- Silence on how the platform is paid; say it in the footer, survey and confirmation (consent draft §1, §7) [capture].
- Unsourced counts, savings and "only" or "biggest" claims; define and date every count and ask counsel about substantiation (reference-matrix §4 item 7) [capture /].
- Unexplained vetting and missing USDOT or MC numbers; link each badge to the method page (mover-verification §4, §7; compliance facts §1) [capture].
- Calling move details non-personal, or a "we" that includes unnamed affiliates; name the buying movers and what they get (lead-lifecycle §5) [capture /privacy/].
- Utility cross-sells without owner and counsel sign-off (consent draft §3; lead-lifecycle §1), and tags that read form fields (site-architecture §8.6) [capture /].
- Errors that drop answers or force a login (lead-lifecycle §1-2) [walk].
- City pages without real coverage and data (gates G1, G4, G5); enter New York through routes and long-distance pages, not "movers nyc" (KD 72) (site-architecture §4.1, §5.3) [semrush].
- A second blog host, uppercase state codes, lifestyle posts, link-only FAQs, relative dates and years in titles; the §8.2 title templates have none (site-architecture §1.2, §2.1-§2.2, §7.3-§7.4) [capture; semrush].
- Duplicate titles, image-only H1s and app links on the mobile first screen (site-architecture §9; visual-direction.md) [capture].

## Gaps and open questions
- Everything after the first screen, including contact, consent and named sellers [walk].
- Footer pages not captured: Agreement, Terms, For Service Providers [capture].
- FMCSA or state registration (one search found none, which proves nothing), and current BBB and complaint records [search].
- Profile and city templates, the sources of the rating and the 45% figure, the full privacy policy, whether tags get form values, robots, sitemaps, keyword lists, backlinks (none in Semrush) and AI answers [capture; semrush backlinks_overview].
- Plan questions: merge "best movers in {city}" into the city page, against site-architecture §2.4 rule 6? Set a guide review interval (§4.4, §7.3) [inference].
