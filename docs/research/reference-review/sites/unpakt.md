# Unpakt: reference review

An online moving marketplace where consumers compare "guaranteed" prices, book a mover and pay Unpakt, which pays the mover after the move [capture /, /how-it-works/]. It is on the list as the clearest example of the book-and-pay model AMC has ruled out, and as a source of price-explainer and first-screen patterns [inference].

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-27. **Evidence:** 9 of 9 pages captured (www.unpakt.com, and blog.unpakt.com marked "blog"); an automated and a guided walk of the home form, both stopped after the first screen; Semrush US, October 2026 (estimates); single-run WebSearch from earlier stages, not re-verified, so search-only claims are marked or left out. **Confidence:** medium, because nothing past the first form screen, and no mover profile or city page, was observed.

## At a glance
- Unpakt presents itself as a place to book and pay: each mover sets a "cost per unit of stuff", a standard formula produces a "Guaranteed price", and Unpakt pays the mover after the move [capture /how-it-works/, /].
- No captured page says how Unpakt itself is paid; the only fee text is a referral fee for partners [capture /partnership/].
- All six captured www pages mark Unpakt up as a MovingCompany rated 4.2 from 36 ratings, and no visible rating was recorded [capture].
- The first screen presets move size and date; the guided walk then failed twice and landed on /sign-in/ [walk /].
- Movers are called "pre screened" and "licensed" with no method, and no USDOT or MC number appears on any captured page [capture].
- About 5,976 estimated organic visits a month, 85% on two URLs, and no top-10 ranking in the 14 SERPs pulled for AMC target terms [semrush domain_rank, top_pages_more, phrase_organic_top10].
- The pay-through model matches the "Booking deposits and payments" add-on still listed in AMC's mvp-scope §5 [capture /; inference].

## Mover side
- Each mover sets its own unit cost and Unpakt's formula sets the consumer's price [capture /how-it-works/]. No captured page covers mover fees, joining rules or screening; the For Service Providers, Agreement and Terms pages were not captured [capture]. A 13% mover fee in 2012 press and commission tiers in a mover help center were found by search but not re-verified [search: betakit.com, third party; unpakt-mover-faq.groovehq.com, site's own].
- Referral partners, including real estate agents, earn a fee on each booked move, "paid monthly", through a form asking for name, title, company, website, email, phone and a message [capture /partnership/, footer]. The pitch also offers clients "an exclusive discount for any moving company th…" (cut off; terms not recorded) [capture /partnership/].
- One blog category page loads an affiliates.unpakt.com widget loader with an affiliate ID [capture blog /category/how-to-before-moving/]. That points to a widget for partner sites, but no partner embed was observed [inference]. Semrush holds no backlink data for unpakt.com [semrush backlinks_overview].
- Consumer offers: a header "Get $20" link, a blog "Get $20 Now!" email form and a home "Refer a friend to Unpakt!" section; their terms were not captured [capture].

## Trust, verification and reviews
- **Vetting.** "Pre screened" (/partnership/) and "licensed" (/faq/, /how-it-works/ meta) come with no explanation of the checks [capture]. v1 reported license, insurance, registration and BBB checks [v1 §2.7]. Help-center screening pages were not re-verified, so their details are not repeated [search: unpakt-faqs.groovehq.com, site's own].
- **Numbers.** No USDOT, DOT or MC text for Unpakt or any mover appears on the nine captured pages [capture]. Mover profiles and city pages exist but were not captured [semrush top_pages_more].
- **Guarantee.** The home page offers a "100% price and service guarantee", says movers "are obligated to honor your guaranteed price", says quotes "are not based on estimates or hourly fees" and promises "no hidden fees" [capture /]. The price appears instantly from the online inventory, before anyone sees the goods; whether movers survey later was not observed [capture /how-it-works/].
- **Claims.** "800,000+ moves booked", "4Million+ miles moved" and "save 45% on your moving costs on average" carry no source or date [capture /], nor do "the only place" [capture /partnership/] and "the the biggest online resource" [capture blog / meta]. v1 traces the 45% to a 2015 study Unpakt commissioned, and a search found an Unpakt blog post on it; neither was re-verified [v1 §2.7; search, site's own page].
- **Ratings.** The 4.2 / 36 markup describes Unpakt itself, even on /sign-in/ and /privacy/ [capture]. A fixed 36 ratings beside 800,000+ bookings looks hard-coded [inference]. Off-site scores found by search were not re-verified, so none is repeated [search: uk.trustpilot.com, consumersadvocate.org, third party].
- **Reviews.** The site promises "real customer reviews" (/ meta) and "real user reviews" (/partnership/), but no captured page explains how reviews are collected, checked or moderated [capture]. v1 said profile reviews are partly pulled from other review portals [v1 §2.7].

## Disclosure and consent
- **Role.** The copy says "our movers" and "Book the Best Moving Companies Online", which presents a booking platform; no captured page says whether Unpakt is a mover, a broker or neither [capture /]. Setting prices and holding payments sits uneasily with v1's report that the Agreement disclaims "involvement or control over the actual transaction" [inference; v1 §2.7].
- **Payment.** Under "Payment Protection": "You don't pay the movers directly. We pay the movers after your move is comp[lete]" [capture /; walk /]. FMCSA's 2023 broker guidance weighs whether an entity handles money between shipper and carrier (compliance facts §2).
- **Consent.** No consent wording was seen, because the walk never reached a contact step [walk]. No consent-certificate script was detected on the nine pages, but the step where one would load was not reached [capture]. An "I want to connect utilities" option sits by the main button; its field type and default were not recorded [capture /].
- **Tags.** Google Tag Manager, Google Ads, Facebook, Zoho PageSense and Cloudflare Insights load [capture]. No cookie-consent host appears, though one could be self-hosted, and the privacy policy points users to browser settings [capture /privacy/].
- **Privacy.** The policy of "Unpakt, LLC" defines "we" to include "its affiliates" and calls household-goods details, including "item or shipment values", non-personal [capture /privacy/]. It allows data from "publicly and commercially available sources" and partners "such as advertising networks", and lists ad IDs and purchasing data; the excerpts do not say what movers receive [capture /privacy/].

## SEO and content
- **Footprint.** 5,976 estimated visits a month from 8,387 keywords; the home page takes 65.72%, /moving-companies/NY/new-york/ 19.29%, and the top 20 URLs 97% [semrush domain_rank, top_pages_more]. No unpakt.com row appears in the 14 SERPs pulled for AMC terms, so it is no rival on Wave 1 terms; those SERPs show Yelp, Thumbtack, Forbes and HireAHelper on Dallas terms [semrush phrase_organic_top10; keyword-research §3].
- **Value.** Traffic cost per visit is about $22.94, roughly twice the next reference site (hireahelper.com, about $10.45) [semrush domain_rank]. One likely reason is that most visits land on two commercial pages; no keyword list was pulled to confirm it [inference].
- **City pages.** New York earns 1,153 visits from 299 keywords; five other city pages earn 9 to 48 each (104 combined), even at KD 37 (Miami) and KD 30 (Columbus) [semrush top_pages_more, phrase_these]. NYC has about 4 times Miami's demand but 24 times its visits, and the evidence does not show why [semrush; site-architecture §5.2]. Two mover profiles earn 16 and 11 visits, and national service pages 9 to 73 [semrush top_pages_more].
- **URLs.** Uppercase state codes mix with lowercase cities (/moving-companies/FL/miami/), profiles carry city and state in the slug (/movers/move-4-less-las-vegas-nv/), and long distance sits at /moving-long/ [semrush top_pages_more]. This corrects v1, which saw no geo URLs on the main domain [v1 §2.7, §5].
- **Two hosts.** The product runs on www with MovingCompany markup; a WordPress blog on blog.unpakt.com has its own navigation and Organization markup [capture]. Intents overlap: a blog "Best Moving Companies in New York City" post, linked as "Best Movers NYC", beside the www NYC page, and a blog Jersey City cost post beside /moving-companies/NJ/jersey-city/ (17 visits) [capture blog /, /category/moving-cost/; semrush].
- **Blog.** Seven blog URLs earn 473 visits (7.9%), about 330 of them on population, museum, restaurant and home-type lists [semrush top_pages_more]. Listings show only relative dates, from "2 years ago" to "4 years ago", and one title ends "in 2023" [capture blog]. The cost category promises costs "in every major city in the US" but shows single-city posts and no method or source [capture blog /category/moving-cost/].
- **Answers and markup.** /faq/ is 212 words of "READ MORE" links without FAQPage markup; no captured page has BreadcrumbList, Article or Person markup or names an author, reviewer or methodology page [capture]. The most quotable shape is the one-line price formula with numbered steps [capture /how-it-works/, /].
- **Hygiene.** /sign-in/ reuses the home title and meta description, the blog H1 is an image, and typos appear in the /partnership/ title, the blog home meta, /category/moving-cost/ and /privacy/ [capture]. Robots directives were not captured [capture].
- **Local facts.** The blog home states a checkable NYC building rule on certificates of insurance for co-ops and condos [capture blog /].
- **Navigation.** The capture recorded link labels, not targets; no header label names a city or state, while "Get $20" and "For Service Providers" sit in the main header [capture].

## Design and UX
- On mobile, "The Moving App" link comes first, ahead of the H1, on all four www pages whose mobile order was recorded; on the home page the "Moving From" field and a support link are also above the fold [capture /, /partnership/, /how-it-works/, /sign-in/].
- On desktop, About Us, FAQ, Services, Contact, Blog, "Get $20", "For Service Providers" and Log In precede the H1; the form's desktop position was not recorded [capture /].
- Explainer pages carry their own "Start!" form, and the home page groups benefits as "Huge Savings", "Payment Protection" and "Quality Moving Companies" [capture /how-it-works/, /faq/; walk /].
- Both address inputs share one element id [walk /]. An undated "The Next Web" press button and years-old blog dates read as stale [capture /, blog].

## What AMC should adopt or adapt
- **Move details first, from any page** [walk /; capture /how-it-works/]. Better: origin and destination after the H1 and lede on mobile and on every explainer; required size options pairing bedrooms with square feet, never preset; a flexible-date option; stops and storage asked early; a unique id and label per field. Serves: quote survey (lead-lifecycle §2 steps 1-5; shared-pricing §6; visual-direction §12).
- **A plain price explainer** [capture /how-it-works/]. Better: a short formula of the estimator's inputs on a versioned method page, shown as a range, never guaranteed or binding, plus numbered steps stating 1 mover = exclusive, 2 or more = shared up to the admin maximum; an optional inventory step can sharpen the lead spec movers receive. Serves: value before contact (site-architecture §6.3 rule 5; reference-matrix §3; counsel review) and "moving cost calculator" (5,400/mo, KD 49) [semrush keyword-research §3].
- **Control instead of a no-calls promise** [capture /]. Better: the consumer picks how many movers and sees their names before agreeing, with a matched-mover preview (how many, who, verification dates). Serves: quote survey (lead-lifecycle §2 steps 7-8).
- **Payment reassurance, turned around** [capture /]. Better: "How AMC makes money" and "Movers vs Brokers" pages saying AMC never prices, books or takes payment, movers pay for the requests they buy and consumers pay their chosen mover directly, repeated in the footer, survey, near quote buttons and on confirmation. Serves: trust pages (consent draft §1, §7).
- **Reassurance as education** [capture /]. Better: binding and non-binding estimates, the 110% rule, safe payment using Protect Your Move's cash and deposit red flags, and FMCSA's Rights and Responsibilities booklet. Serves: trust (compliance facts §1 implication 7, §2).
- **A three-benefit block** [walk /]. Better: only claims AMC can prove (movers-only verification, you choose how many movers, names before consent) and no savings number. Serves: trust (visual-direction §12; compliance facts §2).
- **Promised real reviews** [capture / meta]. Better: evidence labels (verified platform inquiry, verified customer, verified completed move), separate quote and move ratings, a public review methodology, and negative reviews shown with company replies. Serves: reviews (mover-verification §6; compliance facts §4).
- **City data** [capture blog /category/moving-cost/, blog /]. Better: cost ranges only from mover-reported rate cards (at least 5 movers per geography) or cited studies, with n, period, method and "data as of", on the page they describe; AMC lead data only for non-price facts; building access rules as sourced, dated local facts. Serves: real-data rule (site-architecture §4.1 G3-G5, §6.3 rule 2, §7.5).
- **Joining rules for the owner** [inference]. Options: own trucks, which helps screen out brokers, and a minimum operating history, which screens out new or reincarnated carriers. Serves: movers only (mover-verification §1, §3).
- **Partner channel, later** [capture /partnership/]. Better: embedded forms run the same survey, named-mover consent and sharing limit; partner disclosure goes to counsel (compliance facts §4); reward forms stay apart from quote consent (CAN-SPAM, compliance facts §3) and from review sentiment (§465.4). A future reputation widget uses a plain brand link marked sponsored or nofollow, checked against Google's link-spam policy [verify] (mvp-scope §5). Serves: lead-lifecycle §2.

## What AMC should avoid
- Pricing, booking, taking payment, or guaranteeing or discounting movers' prices (discovery brief §1; compliance facts §2) [capture /, /partnership/]. Flag the mvp-scope §5 payments add-on for removal or counsel sign-off (compliance facts §2, §7 implication 4; discovery brief §4), and never imply AMC protects payments.
- Copy that sets movers' prices apart from estimates or promises "no hidden fees" for movers AMC does not control (compliance facts §2; consent draft §6) [capture /].
- Mover-type markup, "our movers" and self-ratings: one Organization @id, MovingCompany only for mover entities, aggregateRating only on profiles with genuine on-page reviews, and a schema lint that fails the build (site-architecture §7.1, §8.5, §9; compliance facts §2, §5) [capture].
- Silence on how the platform is paid; publish mover pricing and rules at /for-movers/ ("buy moving leads", 720/mo, KD 18), and never call reviews "independent" without saying movers pay, a §465.6 risk (compliance facts §4) [capture; semrush keyword-research §3].
- Unsourced counts and savings, and "best", "only", "biggest" or "guaranteed" in titles and trust copy; define and date every count, and ask counsel how FTC substantiation applies (reference-matrix §4 item 7; site-architecture §1.2, §4.6, §6.3 rule 3) [capture /].
- Unexplained vetting: show each mover's USDOT (and MC) with FMCSA links on profiles, in the consent list and on city pages, and link every badge to the method page with a "verified on" date (compliance facts §1 implication 6; mover-verification §4, §7) [capture].
- Calling move details non-personal or sharing with unnamed "affiliates"; name the buying movers, summarize what they receive (lead-lifecycle §5), and make sure counsel's state-privacy review covers move details tied to a contact (backlog line 79) [capture /privacy/].
- Utility or storage cross-sells without an owner and counsel decision (consent draft §3; lead-lifecycle §1), tags that read contact or consent fields (site-architecture §8.6), and lead enrichment from commercial data sources [capture].
- Errors that drop answers or force a login; keep every answer, show a neutral message with retry and support, log failures, and never require an account to request quotes (lead-lifecycle §1-2) [walk].
- City pages without real coverage and data (site-architecture §4.1 G1, G4, G5); enter New York through routes and long-distance pages, not "movers nyc" (KD 72) (§5.3) [semrush].
- A second blog host, uppercase state codes, lifestyle posts, link-only FAQs, FAQPage markup, relative dates and years in titles; use absolute "Last updated" and "Last reviewed" dates (site-architecture §1.2, §2.1-§2.2, §7.3-§7.4, §8.2) [capture; semrush].
- Duplicate titles, image-only H1s, undated press logos, and app or promo links on the mobile first screen (site-architecture §9; visual-direction §12; discovery brief §4) [capture].

## Gaps and open questions
- Everything after the first screen: where contact details are first asked, account rules, how many movers are priced, credentials in the comparison, consent wording and named sellers [walk].
- Uncaptured footer pages (Agreement, Terms, For Service Providers, What is included, Press): arbitration, cancellation, when the card is charged, commission disclosure and v1's "no involvement or control" clause [capture].
- How Unpakt is paid now; FMCSA or state registration (one search found none, which proves nothing); current BBB and complaint records [search].
- Profile and city templates, the source of the 4.2 / 36 markup, the basis of the scale and savings claims, and individual blog posts (bylines, absolute dates, markup) [capture; semrush].
- The utilities option and its data recipient, the full privacy policy, what "our Move affiliates" means, and whether tags receive form values [capture /, /privacy/]. Search summaries, not verified, link the founder to FlatRate Moving; whether related movers appear in comparisons is unknown [search].
- Robots, sitemaps, canonicals and AI-crawler rules; Semrush keyword lists, backlinks and trends; direct AI-engine answers [capture; semrush].
- Owner questions: merge "best movers in {city}" into the city page, against site-architecture §2.4 rule 6? Set a guide review interval tied to "Last reviewed" (§4.4 already prunes stale data; §7.3)? Keep the payments add-on? [inference]
