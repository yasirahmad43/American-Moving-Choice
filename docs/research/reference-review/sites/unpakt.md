# Unpakt: reference review

An online moving marketplace that prices moves from movers' unit rates, takes the consumer's payment and pays the mover afterward [capture /how-it-works/, /]; it is the clearest example of the book-and-pay model AMC has ruled out [inference].

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-27. **Evidence:** 9 of 9 pages captured (www.unpakt.com; "blog" marks blog.unpakt.com); automated and guided walks of the home form, both stopped after the first screen; Semrush US (domain rank, top 20 pages); limited WebSearch, not re-verified. **Confidence:** medium: the pages and Semrush data are solid, but the price comparison, contact and consent steps, profiles and city pages were not seen.

## At a glance
- Each mover sets a "cost per unit of stuff", Unpakt's formula sets the price, and Unpakt pays the mover after the move [capture /how-it-works/, /].
- No captured page says how Unpakt itself is paid [capture].
- All six www pages mark Unpakt up as a MovingCompany rated 4.2 from 36 ratings; no visible rating was recorded [capture].
- The first screen presets move size and date; the walk then failed twice and landed on /sign-in/ [walk /].
- "Pre screened" and "licensed" come with no method and no USDOT or MC numbers [capture /partnership/, /faq/].
- About 5,976 estimated organic visits a month, 85% on two URLs, and no ranking in the 14 SERPs pulled for AMC's terms [semrush domain_rank, top_pages_more, phrase_organic_top10].

## Mover side
Partners, including real estate agents, earn a referral fee per booked move, paid monthly [capture /partnership/, footer]. The pitch also offers clients "an exclusive discount for any moving company" (excerpt cut off) [capture /partnership/]. A blog category page loads an affiliates.unpakt.com widget loader [capture blog /category/how-to-before-moving/], which suggests a partner widget, though no embed was seen [inference]. "Get $20" offers appear with no terms captured [capture]. The For Service Providers, Agreement and Terms pages were not captured [capture]. A 13% mover fee in 2012 press and help-center commission tiers were not re-verified [search: betakit.com, third party; unpakt-mover-faq.groovehq.com, site's own].

## Trust, verification and reviews
- **Vetting.** v1 reported license, insurance, registration and BBB checks [v1 §2.7]; a help center reportedly describes screening but was not re-verified [search: unpakt-faqs.groovehq.com, site's own]. No USDOT, DOT or MC text appears on the nine pages [capture].
- **Guarantee.** The home page offers a "100% price and service guarantee" and says movers "are obligated to honor your guaranteed price" [capture /]. The price comes instantly from the online inventory, before anyone sees the goods [capture /how-it-works/]. Quotes "are not based on estimates or hourly fees" [capture /].
- **Claims.** "800,000+ moves booked" and "save 45% on your moving costs on average" have no source or date [capture /]; v1 traced the 45% to a 2015 study Unpakt commissioned [v1 §2.7]. "The only place" and "the the biggest online resource" are unsupported [capture /partnership/, blog / meta].
- **Ratings.** The 4.2 / 36 markup describes Unpakt itself, even on /sign-in/ and /privacy/ [capture], and sits oddly beside 800,000+ bookings [inference]. Off-site scores were not re-verified, so none is repeated [search: uk.trustpilot.com, consumersadvocate.org, third party].
- **Reviews.** "Real customer reviews" are promised, but no captured page explains how reviews are collected or checked [capture / meta, /partnership/]. v1 said profile reviews are "partly pulled from other review portals" [v1 §2.7].

## Disclosure and consent
- **Role.** The home page says "our movers" but otherwise presents a booking platform; no captured page says whether Unpakt is a mover, broker or neither [capture /]. Pricing and holding payments sit uneasily with v1's report that the Agreement disclaims "involvement or control over the actual transaction" [inference; v1 §2.7].
- **Payment.** "You don't pay the movers directly" [capture /]. AMC's mvp-scope §5 lists booking deposits and payments as an add-on candidate, which would put AMC in the money flow that 2023 FMCSA guidance weighs (compliance facts §2) [inference].
- **Consent.** None was seen; the walk never reached contact [walk]. No consent-certificate script was detected, but its usual step was not reached [capture]. An "I want to connect utilities" option sits by the main button, type and default unrecorded [capture /].
- **Tags.** Google Tag Manager, Google Ads, Facebook, Zoho PageSense and Cloudflare Insights load; no cookie-consent host appears [capture].
- **Privacy.** The policy, for a site "operated by Unpakt, LLC", calls "item or shipment values" non-personal, may take data from "commercially available sources" and ad networks, and leaves cookies to browser settings [capture /privacy/]. The excerpts do not say what movers receive [capture /privacy/].

## SEO and content
- **Footprint.** 5,976 estimated visits from 8,387 keywords: 65.72% to the home page, 19.29% to /moving-companies/NY/new-york/ [semrush domain_rank, top_pages_more]. Unpakt is no search rival on AMC's Wave 1 terms [semrush phrase_organic_top10].
- **Value.** About $22.94 per visit ($137,111 / 5,976), twice hireahelper.com, the next highest [semrush domain_rank]; landing on commercial pages may explain it [inference].
- **City pages.** Only New York earns real traffic (1,153 visits); five other city pages earn 9 to 48 each, even at KD 37 (Miami) and KD 30 (Columbus) [semrush top_pages_more, phrase_these]. NYC has about 4 times Miami's demand but 24 times its visits; the evidence does not show why [inference; site-architecture §5.2].
- **URLs.** Uppercase state codes mix with lowercase cities (/moving-companies/FL/miami/), profiles sit at /movers/{company}-{city}-{st}/, and long distance at /moving-long/ [semrush]. This corrects v1, which saw no geo URLs [v1 §2.7].
- **Two hosts.** www (MovingCompany markup) and the blog (Organization) use different navigation [capture]. NYC intent overlaps: the city page and a blog "Best Moving Companies in New York City" post [capture blog /; semrush]. No header label names a city or state [capture].
- **Blog.** Seven blog URLs earn 473 visits, about 330 on population, museum, restaurant and home-type lists [semrush]. Dates are relative ("2 years ago" to "4 years ago"), one title says "in 2023", and cost posts cite no source [capture blog].
- **Profiles and service pages** earn 9 to 73 visits each [semrush].
- **Markup.** /faq/ is 212 words of "READ MORE" links, and no captured page has an author or FAQPage, BreadcrumbList, Article or Person markup [capture]. The best quotable shape is the /how-it-works/ price formula [capture].
- **Hygiene.** /sign-in/ reuses the home title and meta, the blog H1 is an image, four typos appear, and robots directives were not captured [capture].

## Design and UX
- On mobile, "The Moving App" link comes before the H1 on four www pages; the home "Moving From" field and a support link sit above the fold [capture /, /partnership/, /how-it-works/, /sign-in/].
- On desktop, promo, provider and login links precede the H1; the form's position was not recorded [capture /].
- Explainer pages carry their own From and To form, and three benefit blocks lead with "Huge Savings" [capture /how-it-works/, /faq/; walk /].

## What AMC should adopt or adapt
- **Move details first** [walk /]. Better: no presets, a required size pairing bedrooms with square feet, a flexible-date option, and extra stops and storage asked early. Serves lead-lifecycle §2 and shared-pricing §6.
- **Price explainer** [capture /how-it-works/]. Better: a plain formula of the estimator's inputs on a versioned method page, shown as a range, with numbered steps stating the 1-mover exclusive, 2-or-more shared rule. Serves site-architecture §6.3 rule 5 and "moving cost calculator" (5,400/mo, KD 49) [semrush].
- **Control, not "no calls"** [capture /]. Better: the consumer picks how many movers and sees their names and verification dates first. Serves lead-lifecycle §2 steps 7-8.
- **City cost content** [capture blog /category/moving-cost/]. Better: ranges only from mover rate cards (5+ movers) or cited studies, lead data for non-price facts, on the page it describes. Serves site-architecture §6.3 rule 2, §7.5.
- **Local rules as facts** [capture blog /]. Better: sourced, dated building rules such as certificates of insurance. Serves gate G3 (site-architecture §4.1).
- **Quote start on every explainer** [capture]. Better: route fields right after the H1 on mobile, with benefits AMC can prove. Serves visual-direction.md.
- **Joining rules for the owner to weigh** [inference]. Own trucks screen out brokers; a minimum operating history screens out new or reincarnated carriers. Serves mover-verification §1, §3.
- **Partner channel, later** [capture /partnership/]. Better: the same survey, named-mover consent and sharing limit, with disclosure as a counsel question. Serves lead-lifecycle §2; compliance facts §3-§4.
- **Reassurance as education** [capture /]. Better: estimate types, the 110% rule, safe payment and FMCSA links. Serves compliance facts §1-§2.

## What AMC should avoid
- Pricing, booking, taking payment or discounting movers' prices (discovery brief §1; compliance facts §2) [capture /, /partnership/]; flag the mvp-scope §5 payments add-on for counsel.
- Guaranteeing movers' prices, "no hidden fees", or "not estimates" copy (compliance facts §2) [capture /].
- MovingCompany or LocalBusiness markup on AMC, "our movers", and self-ratings; keep one Organization @id and a CI schema lint (site-architecture §7.1, §8.5, §9; compliance facts §5) [capture].
- Silence on how the platform is paid; say it in the footer, survey and confirmation (consent draft §1, §7) [capture].
- Unsourced counts, savings and "only" or "biggest" claims; define counts with an "as of" date (reference-matrix §4 item 7) [capture /].
- Unexplained vetting and missing USDOT or MC numbers; link every badge to the method page (mover-verification §4, §7; compliance facts §1) [capture].
- Calling move details non-personal or sharing with unnamed "affiliates"; name buying movers and summarize what they get (lead-lifecycle §5) [capture /privacy/].
- Utility cross-sells without an owner and counsel decision (consent draft §3; lead-lifecycle §1), and tags that read form fields (site-architecture §8.6) [capture /].
- Errors that drop answers or force a login (lead-lifecycle §1-2) [walk].
- City pages without deep supply; keep gates G1, G4 and G5 and enter New York through routes, not "movers nyc" (KD 72) (site-architecture §4.1, §5.3) [semrush].
- A second blog host, uppercase state codes, lifestyle posts, link-only FAQs, relative dates and years in titles, which the §8.2 templates already omit (site-architecture §1.2, §2.1-§2.2, §7.3) [capture; semrush].
- Duplicate titles, image-only H1s and app links on the mobile first screen (site-architecture §9; discovery brief §4) [capture].

## Gaps and open questions
- Everything after the first screen, including contact, consent and named sellers [walk].
- The Agreement, Terms and For Service Providers pages, and commission, refund and card terms [capture].
- Registration: one search found no FMCSA record, which proves nothing; BBB and complaint history are unchecked [search].
- Profile and city templates, the rating's source, the 45% study and the full privacy policy [capture; semrush].
- Whether tags receive form values, and what the utilities option shares [capture].
- Robots, sitemaps, keyword lists and backlinks; AI answer engines were not queried [capture; semrush].
- Plan questions: should "best movers in {city}" merge into the city page, against site-architecture §2.4 rule 6? Add a guide review interval (§4.4, §7.3) [inference].
