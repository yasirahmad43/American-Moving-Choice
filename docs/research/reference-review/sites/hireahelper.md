# HireAHelper (company profile and marketplace): reference review

A marketplace where consumers compare hourly moving crews and local full-service movers, then book and pay through HireAHelper's checkout [capture /how-it-works/, /services/]. It is on the list because the owner pointed to one of its company profiles, a structure AMC's discovery brief calls "the best model" (00-discovery-brief.md §3 item 4).

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-27. **Evidence:** 13 of 14 pages captured (/affiliate/ blocked by Cloudflare, HTTP 403); an automated walk from the A-1 Moving profile reached the mover list before any contact step, while the homepage walk stopped at a custom widget; Semrush US (October 2026); standard WebSearch, with several results not re-checked. **Confidence:** medium, because the profile, city pages and pre-contact flow are well captured, but booking, checkout consent, the terms and provider fees were not observed.

## At a glance
- The consumer compares providers, picks one team and pays through HireAHelper; the flow runs under /checkoutv2/ [capture /, /how-it-works/; walk].
- From a profile, two short screens reach the mover list without any contact details; an email box there is optional and skippable [walk /checkoutv2/helperlist/].
- No consent wording appeared up to that list; booking and checkout were not walked [walk].
- Its strongest trust features (reviews from paid moves, booked-move counts, the $1,000 guarantee, the advocate role) rest on running the booking and taking payment, which AMC does not do [inference]. This revises the discovery brief's "best model" view (00-discovery-brief.md §3 item 4).
- A five-point vetting checklist is published, but the captured excerpts name no USDOT, MC or state-license check and show no last-checked date [capture /how-it-works/, profiles].
- Counts and ratings change between pages and carry no "as of" date [capture].
- About 125,334 estimated organic visits a month; ten city pages in the top 15 bring 24.6% of them [semrush domain_rank, top_pages].

- No date was typed, yet the form came pre-filled and carried 11/3/2026 into the list header [walk]. Service pages promise movers "actually available on your date", so a date the consumer never chose can shape the list [capture /services/moving-companies/; inference].
- The consumer picks one team ("Choose the team that fits and book online"), which implies no broadcast, though booking was not observed [capture /how-it-works/; inference]. "Best Match" is the first sort option, and the "How the match works" block was cut off, so whether fees affect the order is unknown [walk; capture /how-it-works/].
- The Georgia-to-Florida test ran as "No Truck, Labor Only" and listed other companies first [walk]. The profile form offers only labor services and the ZIPs were outside A-1's area, so neither result is conclusive [walk].

## Mover side
Movers join through footer links such as "Get Listed on HireAHelper", "Provider Sign Up", "Provider Software" and "Movers Academy" [capture footer]. Each booking goes to the one team the consumer picks, so there is no sharing cap to manage [inference]. The homepage says providers "advertise" on HireAHelper, but no captured page says what they pay [capture /]. Fee statements found in search (an undated third-party blog and a garbled excerpt of HireAHelper's provider page) were not re-checked, so no figures are repeated here [search: sidehusl.com, third party; hireahelper.com/providers/moving-company-software/, site's own]. The footer links "Porch Group", lists ISN, EIG, Simple Moving Labor and MovingPlace under "PARTNERS" and reads "© HireAHelper, LLC." [capture footer]. Third parties report that Porch bought HireAHelper in 2019, while a search summary of /about/ places a Porch partnership in 2018; neither was re-checked [search: geekwire.com, app.dealroom.co, third party; hireahelper.com/about/, site's own].

## Trust, verification and reviews
- **Vetting.** Providers pass a "five-point vetting process before they can take their first booking" [capture /how-it-works/]. Three points were captured: an owner background check, verified W-9, registration and payment details, and "10+ reviews and a 4-star rating" [capture /how-it-works/]. Profiles promise ongoing monitoring, and full-service bookings need "active COI on file" (that list was cut off) [capture profiles, /movers/].
- **Not shown.** The excerpts name no USDOT, MC or state-license check and no last-checked date, but two vetting points and the "License & Insurance" page were not captured [capture]. v1 reported state-license checks for full-service jobs [v1 §2.4]. Both profiles' "Credentials" block lists only the self-declared "I Offer Uniformed Crew Members", and "Licensed & insured crews" names no license [capture profiles, /services/moving-companies/]. /about/ says "independently vetted", which is ambiguous because HireAHelper does the vetting [capture /about/].
- **Badge.** The review threshold sits inside the "Vetted & Verified" status (the first H2 on both profiles), so a business with no reviews cannot qualify [capture /how-it-works/, profiles]. Reviews are collected only after paid bookings, yet a review history is needed before the first booking, and the excerpts do not say where it comes from [capture /movers/, profiles].
- **Reviews.** "Every review on HireAHelper comes from a real, paid move" [capture /movers/]. Profiles show "Unedited, unfiltered reviews" with mover replies, including to critical reviews [capture /movers/los-angeles_ca/excellent-movers-los-angeles/]. /movers/ has a "How We Collect and Verify Reviews" block, and profiles a shorter one [capture]. City pages feature selected reviews with no stated rule [capture /movers/dalton_ga/]. Review dates were not in the excerpts; v1 and a search summary date A-1's visible reviews to 2015-2016 [v1 §2.4; search: hireahelper.com/movers/dalton_ga/a-1-moving, site's own, not re-checked].
- **Guarantees.** The same $1,000 is "damage coverage" on / and a "Service Guarantee" on /services/moving-companies/ [capture]. "The lowest price on your move, guaranteed" (/services/) narrows to the same service, mover and date "on our platform" (/moving-cost-calculator/) [capture]. A search summary of the FAQ ties coverage to an inventory form, which would conflict with "no detailed inventory list required" on / [search: hireahelper.com/help/faq/, site's own, not re-checked; capture /].
- **Numbers.** "1,700+ vetted movers" (/) sits beside "Showing 1848 vetted movers" (/movers/, before a cut-off definition) [capture]. Ratings read "4.6 on 9,100+ reviews" (/, city pages), "4.6 out of 5 with 13k reviews" (/services/, /how-it-works/, /about/, /movers/) and an unsourced "4.8/5" (/about/) [capture]. Excellent Movers shows 827 reviews and 2,513 moves on its profile, but 825 and 2512 on its city page [capture]. None carries an "as of" date [capture].
- **Profiles and cards.** A-1's header reads "Up to 0 miles" [capture /movers/dalton_ga/a-1-moving/]. Both profiles share a meta template calling the mover "one of the top-rated" in its own voice, a "Moving with with" heading and an H1 framed as "HireAHelper's Review Of" the company, though customers supply the ratings [capture]. City cards print mover-written claims and typos unedited [capture /movers/los-angeles_ca/, /movers/tallahassee_fl/].
- **City counts.** "Compare the Best 10 Vetted Movers in Tallahassee, FL" counts movers based in Orlando and Newton, GA, and Dalton's list includes Cumming and Atlanta movers [capture]. Dalton's "21+ verified customer reviews nearby" sits above a mover with 1046 [capture /movers/dalton_ga/]. v1 recorded 7 Dalton movers; the capture shows 20 [v1 §2.4; capture].

## Disclosure and consent
- **Role.** The clearest role line is in body text: "HireAHelper is a national marketplace for booking local, vetted movers online." [capture /services/]. /services/moving-companies/ opens by saying it "connects you with vetted local moving companies", but no role statement appeared inside the flow [capture; walk].
- **Money.** No captured page or footer link says how HireAHelper makes money [capture]. It promises to act "as your advocate, stepping in if something goes wrong" [capture /movers/dalton_ga/].
- **Consent.** Up to the list there was no consent checkbox, call or text consent, automated-technology or "not a condition of purchase" wording, or named seller; the walker searched each step for these terms [walk]. No consent-certificate script was detected [capture]. Terms and Privacy are linked but were not captured, and search did not surface them [capture footer; search].
- **Data.** Captured pages load FullStory session replay, MNTN ad retargeting, Optimizely, HubSpot, Tidio and Sentry, and the footer has "Do not sell my information" [capture]. Whether these run on checkout steps was not recorded, and the first checkout URL carries ZIPs and dates in its query string [capture; walk].
- **Phone.** A phone number is the first element in all six recorded mobile above-the-fold lists, and it changes between pages, viewports and sessions, which suggests call tracking [capture]. /how-it-works/ promises "No spam, no surprises, no calling around for quotes." [capture].
- **Payment timing.** Pages say "pay only when the job is done", while v1 said the card is charged about a day before; they may describe different steps [capture /how-it-works/; v1 §2.4; inference].

## SEO and content
- **Scale.** About 125,334 visits, 80,203 keywords and $1,309,125 traffic value a month, or $10.45 per visit site-wide (move.org $5.60, moving.com $6.24) [semrush domain_rank]. Authority Score 48 and 11,736 referring domains; 96% of 1,983,198 backlinks are nofollow, so referring domains are the better yardstick [semrush backlinks_overview; inference].
- **City pages.** Ten city pages in the top 15 bring 30,820 visits (24.6%), led by /movers/los-angeles_ca/ with 8,915 [semrush top_pages]. /movers/dallas_tx/ ranks #10 for "moving companies dallas" and #8 and #10 for "movers dallas", beside Yelp, Thumbtack and Forbes [semrush phrase_organic_top10]. keyword-research §1 says city pages "drive most traffic"; the data show the largest family in the top 15 and cannot show a majority, since 57% of traffic sits outside it [semrush].
- **Few-keyword pages.** Tallahassee, Beckley and Oshkosh earn 142-162 visits per keyword, against about 7 for Los Angeles [semrush top_pages]. A few high-volume, possibly non-local queries may drive them, so they do not yet support keyword-research §2's view that smaller metros are "achievable" [inference].
- **Template.** The three captured city pages share their first 12 H2s with the city swapped (one differs on Tallahassee), one meta template and repeated intro and advocacy paragraphs; the live mover cards clearly differ, and cost sections and FAQ answers were not captured [capture].
- **Other drivers.** The #2 page, /services/moving-companies/ (7,969 visits), is a national service pillar, not a "best of" list, so keyword-research §2 misfiles it [semrush top_pages; capture]. U-Haul, PODS and container guides are the only content pages in the top 15 (6.1%) [semrush top_pages].
- **Long distance.** HireAHelper is absent from all six measured long-distance, interstate and route top 10s, and none of its top 15 pages is a route, state or long-distance page [semrush]. It sells long distance as labor "at both ends"; a "See our long-distance movers" link was not followed [capture /services/, /services/moving-companies/]. That leaves AMC's planned city long-distance and route pages without this rival (site-architecture §5) [inference].
- **Structure.** Cities use flat /movers/{city}_{st}/ slugs with profiles beneath and a state level in the breadcrumb [capture /movers/los-angeles_ca/]. The footer holds policy and provider links, not city links; cities are reached through /movers/ and a homepage top-cities block [capture]. Profile titles carry city keywords [capture], which may compete with city pages [inference].
- **Markup.** Profiles carry MovingCompany markup whose aggregateRating matches the visible values [capture]. HireAHelper's own pages show Organization and Service nodes plus a type cut off as "Produc…" (/services/moving-companies/, /movers/) or "Pro…" (city pages); the leading Organization node has no direct rating, and the rest cannot be seen [capture].
- **Content.** /advice/ shows named authors, dates and answer-first excerpts; no article was captured [capture /advice/]. The calculator asks for no contact details and publishes a method, but says "not estimates" under a title promising "an Accurate Moving Estimate" [capture /moving-cost-calculator/]. It is outside the top 15 pages and the top 10 for "moving cost calculator" [semrush].

## Design and UX
On mobile the homepage opens with the phone number, logo and Trustpilot rating, then the H1 and two large service buttons [capture /]. On desktop, navigation with a trust link ("Why Book with HireAHelper?") comes first, under a promo-code banner [capture /]. Mobile profiles show phone, logo, breadcrumbs, H1, rating and radius, with no quote form among the first recorded elements [capture]. Two images near the top have empty alt text [capture /]. City cards list rating, review count, base city, moves booked and the mover's text, then hourly terms ("$90/hr After 2 movers, 2 hr min") and "Select Movers" [capture /movers/tallahassee_fl/, /movers/dalton_ga/].

## What AMC should adopt or adapt
- **Value before contact** [walk]. Better: after move details and the coverage pre-check, preview the matched movers (count, names, USDOT, verification dates, review summaries) without prices; ask for a date or flexible range with nothing pre-filled; keep any "save my progress" email optional and apart from consent; reword the planned "See available movers" button, which echoes HireAHelper's phrase in v1. Serves the quote survey (lead-lifecycle §2 steps 3, 5; visual-direction §5.3; compliance facts §2).
- **Consumer choice** [capture /how-it-works/]. Better: show the eligible named movers, let the consumer deselect any before consent within the company-count limit, explain the order, and label any paid placement "Sponsored". Serves named-mover consent (consent draft §5; compliance facts §2-§4).
- **A role line** [capture /services/]. Better: put AMC's role statement beside the hero button and inside the survey, add a footer "How we make money" link, and back its "licensed" claim with a link to what AMC checked. Serves the trust pages (consent draft §1, §7).
- **A vetting checklist** [capture /how-it-works/, /movers/]. Better: one /how-we-verify-movers/ page listing AMC's exact checks, how often they repeat and what is not checked, linked from every badge, plus "verified on {date}" and FMCSA links on each profile. Serves Preferred Movers and "usdot number lookup" (4,400/mo, KD 18) (mover-verification §3-§5; site-architecture §3.2) [semrush phrase_these].
- **Open reviews with replies** [capture]. Better: AMC's labels (Verified platform inquiry, Verified customer, Verified completed move) explained on /review-methodology/, never "paid move" or a mover-confirmed label; platform-sent invitations, a portal reply workflow, and featured reviews picked by a neutral rule. Serves reviews (mover-verification §6; compliance facts §4).
- **Profile and card anatomy** [capture]. Better: only evidenced fields (verification date, review counts by label, member since, latest review date), no placeholders, "based in" apart from "serves this area", neutral generated meta, MovingCompany markup with USDOT, and a request button that opens the standard survey naming that mover first under the normal sharing and consent rules. Serves profiles (mover-verification §5; site-architecture §4.5, §8.2, §8.5).
- **City pages carried by inventory** [inference]. Better: count only Approved movers with explicit county or ZIP coverage, pass G1 before publishing, keep shared explainers on one canonical page, attribute and moderate any mover-written local tips, and define how one mover's card on many pages counts toward G7. Serves the no-doorway rule (site-architecture §4.1; compliance facts §5).
- **Service pillar and ungated calculator** [capture; semrush]. Better: model AMC's national service pillars on the pillar's structure; give the calculator ranges, never "estimates", with a dated method naming the data behind each output. Serves SEO and value before contact (site-architecture §1.2, §6.3).
- **Bylines and dates** [capture /advice/]. Better: add a named reviewer, inline sources and author pages, and put a year in a title only with a real refresh. Serves content quality and AI answers (site-architecture §7.2-§7.4).
- **One source of truth** [capture]. Better: every count and rating from one data service with its source and "as of" date, third-party ratings kept apart, and one support number in the footer and Organization markup. Serves trust pages and entity data (00-discovery-brief.md §8; site-architecture §7.1).
- **Provider tools beside sign-up** [capture footer]. Better: bundle portal tools and training for retention, and publish AMC's lead prices and credit rules on /for-movers/. Serves the company portal (shared-pricing-and-credits.md).

## What AMC should avoid
- Booking, taking payment and "book" wording, and claims built on them: paid-move reviews, booked-move counts, guarantees (compliance facts §2; discovery brief §1) [capture]. This says nothing about HireAHelper's own status, since it mainly sells labor [inference].
- Guarantee, coverage and price-match headlines; explain released value versus full value protection with FMCSA links instead (compliance facts §1 implication 7) [capture].
- Ratings inside the trust badge; Preferred rests on credentials only, with an honest "New to our network" state (mover-verification §4-§5) [capture].
- A phone-first header, a "specialists" advice line, promo banners and absolute no-spam promises; AMC has no sales or advice line and should state the consumer's cap and who may call (consent draft §6; mover-verification §7) [capture].
- "Best" and out-of-area movers in city counts, and radius coverage (site-architecture §4.1, §8.2; lead-lifecycle §3) [capture].
- Template superlatives, first-person meta, "our review of" headings and unedited mover claims (mover-verification §5) [capture].
- Availability claims without real calendars, and pre-filled dates (lead-lifecycle §2-§3; visual-direction §13.7) [walk].
- Ad and replay scripts on survey, contact and consent screens, and move details in URLs (site-architecture §1.2; consent draft §5) [capture; inference].
- Sharing survey data with partner brands without separate consent, or co-branded entry points without the role line and disclosure (consent draft §3, §7) [capture footer].
- DIY truck and container guides in Wave 1 (keyword-research §3) [semrush].
- Copying its markup for AMC's own pages: no self-rating, and no FAQPage markup, since FAQ rich results were retired on 2026-05-07 (compliance facts §5; site-architecture §8.5) [capture].

## Gaps and open questions
- Booking and checkout: where name, phone and payment are asked, and any consent or arbitration text [walk].
- Terms, Privacy, guarantee, Complaints & Claims and License & Insurance pages; /affiliate/ (blocked) and partner pages under /a/ [capture].
- Provider fees and onboarding, and whether fees affect "Best Match", city order or featured reviews [capture].
- HireAHelper's own FMCSA status: a third-party review lists USDOT 3214531 as a "moving labor marketplace", unchecked because fmcsa.dot.gov is blocked here [search: goodmigrations.com, third party]. Whether full-service providers' USDOT numbers and state licenses are checked or shown [capture].
- Review dates, sort order, removal rules and invitation coverage [capture].
- The BBB profile is unconfirmed; eight searches found no FTC, FMCSA or attorney general action, which proves nothing; Porch's TCPA proceedings after 2022 are unknown [search].
- Who owns the PARTNERS brands and what data moves between them; the evidence pack's "sister brands" wording is not in the capture and should be corrected [capture].
- Full JSON-LD, canonicals, robots, sitemaps, state hubs, city cost sections, articles and tables; keyword lists behind the few-keyword pages; AI answer engines [capture; semrush].
- Whether a profile start puts that company first, and where "See our long-distance movers" leads [walk].
- Owner decisions: how to price a request a mover's own prospect submits from its AMC profile (HireAHelper appears to charge less for these; not re-checked); how to match a "Loading help only" request with different origin and destination, which lead-lifecycle §3 would class as interstate (mvp-scope §6); and what the state crumb links to when a city passes its gate but its state hub does not (site-architecture §3.3) [inference].
- Not re-confirmed from v1: the $85/hr profile price, the $0.60/lb line on the Dalton page and the /georgia-moving-companies/ state hub [v1 §2.4].
