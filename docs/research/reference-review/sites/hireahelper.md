# HireAHelper (company profile and marketplace): reference review

A booking marketplace for hourly moving crews and local full-service moves that takes payment through its own checkout [capture /services/, /how-it-works/]; it is on the list because the owner named its A-1 Moving profile, a profile structure AMC's discovery brief calls "the best model" (00-discovery-brief.md §3 item 4).

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-27. **Evidence:** 13 of 14 pages captured (/affiliate/ blocked by Cloudflare); one automated walk from the profile to the mover list, reached before any contact details, with email optional (the homepage walk stalled on a custom widget); Semrush US, October 2026; standard WebSearch, whose search-only claims could not be re-checked, so they are hedged or left out. **Confidence:** medium, because the profile, city pages and pre-contact flow are well captured, but booking, checkout consent, terms and provider fees were not seen.

## At a glance
- Consumers compare providers, choose one team, then book and pay through HireAHelper's checkout [capture /how-it-works/, /movers/; walk].
- From a profile, two short screens reach a mover list with no contact details; the email box there can be skipped [walk /checkoutv2/helperlist/].
- No consent wording appeared up to that list; booking was not walked [walk].
- Its strongest trust signals (paid-move reviews, booked-move counts, the $1,000 guarantee, the advocate role) rest on running the booking and taking payment, which AMC does not do, so this revises the discovery brief's "best model" view [inference].
- Vetting is published, but the captured excerpts name no USDOT, MC or state-license check and show no last-checked date [capture /how-it-works/, profiles].
- Counts and ratings differ between pages, with no "as of" date [capture].
- About 125,334 estimated organic visits a month; city pages are the largest family among its top 15 pages [semrush domain_rank, top_pages].
- It appears in none of six measured long-distance, interstate or route top 10s [semrush phrase_organic_top10].

- **Pre-filled date.** No date was typed, yet 11/3/2026 reached the list as both dates [walk]. Pages promise movers "actually available on your date" (/services/moving-companies/), so a date the consumer never chose shapes the list [capture; walk].
- **One chosen team.** "Choose the team that fits and book online" (/how-it-works/) implies no lead broadcast, though booking was not seen [capture; inference]. "Best Match" is listed first, and the "How the match works" block was cut off, so whether the order is explained, or shaped by fees, is unknown [walk; capture /how-it-works/].
- **Test limits.** The Georgia-to-Florida test ran as "No Truck, Labor Only" and listed other companies first [walk]. The profile form offers only labor services and the ZIPs were outside A-1's area, so neither result is conclusive [walk].

## Mover side
Footer links invite movers to get listed, sign up, log in, use provider software and join "Movers Academy", whose content was not captured [capture footer]. Each booking goes to one chosen provider, so there is no sharing cap to manage [inference]. Providers "advertise on HireAHelper" (/), but no captured page says what they pay [capture]. Fee figures came only from an undated third-party blog and a garbled provider-page excerpt, neither re-checked, so none is repeated [search: https://sidehusl.com/hireahelper/, third party; https://www.hireahelper.com/providers/moving-company-software/, site's own]. Labor-only crews without USDOT and MC cannot join AMC, so AMC's labor-only requests depend on approved carriers that also sell labor (mvp-scope §6) [inference].

## Trust, verification and reviews
- **Vetting.** A "five-point vetting process" comes before a provider's first booking; three points were captured: an owner background check, verified W-9, registration and payment details, and "10+ reviews and a 4-star rating" [capture /how-it-works/]. Profiles promise ongoing monitoring, and full-service bookings need an active liability insurance certificate (that list was cut off) [capture profiles, /movers/].
- **Gaps in the excerpts.** No USDOT, MC or state-license check and no last-checked date appear, but two vetting points and the "License & Insurance" page were not captured, so this does not prove there is no license check [capture]. v1 reported state-license checks for full-service jobs [v1 §2.4]. /about/ calls the labor "independently vetted", which is ambiguous, since HireAHelper does the vetting [capture /about/].
- **Credentials.** Profile "Credentials" list one self-declared attribute in the first person ("I Offer Uniformed Crew Members"), and "Licensed & insured crews" (/services/moving-companies/) names no license [capture profiles].
- **Badge.** The review threshold sits inside "Vetted & Verified", so a business with no reviews cannot qualify [capture /how-it-works/, profiles]. Reviews come only from paid bookings, yet a review history is required before the first booking, and where those reviews come from is unstated [capture /movers/, /movers/dalton_ga/a-1-moving/].
- **Reviews.** "Every review on HireAHelper comes from a real, paid move" (/movers/); that page explains how reviews are collected, and profiles carry a shorter version [capture]. Profiles show "unedited" reviews with public mover replies, even to critical reviews [capture /movers/los-angeles_ca/excellent-movers-los-angeles/]. City pages feature selected reviews, and the excerpts do not say how they are picked or dated [capture /movers/dalton_ga/].
- **Guarantees.** One $1,000 promise is "damage coverage" on / and a "Service Guarantee" on /services/moving-companies/ [capture]. "The lowest price on your move, guaranteed" (/services/) narrows to the "same service, mover, and date on our platform" (/moving-cost-calculator/) [capture]. A FAQ search summary, not re-checked, says coverage needs an inventory form, which would clash with "no detailed inventory list required" (/) [search: https://www.hireahelper.com/help/faq/, site's own; capture].
- **Numbers.** Network size reads "1,700+" (/) and "Showing 1848 vetted movers" (/movers/) [capture]. Trustpilot-badged ratings cite "9,100+" or "13k" reviews by page, beside an unsourced "4.8/5 average rating" (/about/) [capture]. One mover's review and move counts differ between its profile and city page [capture /movers/los-angeles_ca/].
- **Profiles and cards.** A-1 shows "Up to 0 miles" and "Open Today" [capture /movers/dalton_ga/a-1-moving/], while v1 and an unchecked search summary date its visible reviews to 2015-2016 [v1 §2.4; search]. Both profiles share a "top-rated" meta template in the mover's voice and a "Moving with with" heading [capture profiles]. City cards print mover claims unedited, such as "As an Industry Leader in Transportation and Relocation Services." (/movers/los-angeles_ca/) [capture].
- **City counts.** "Compare the Best 10 Vetted Movers in Tallahassee, FL" counts movers based in Orlando and in Newton, Georgia [capture /movers/tallahassee_fl/]. v1 saw 7 Dalton movers; the capture shows 20 [v1 §2.4; capture /movers/dalton_ga/].

## Disclosure and consent
- **Role.** The clearest role line is body text, "a national marketplace for booking local, vetted movers online" (/services/), though /services/moving-companies/ opens with "connects you with vetted local moving companies" [capture]. No role statement appeared in the flow, and the footer has no how-we-make-money link [walk; capture]. City pages cast HireAHelper as the consumer's "advocate, stepping in if something goes wrong" (/movers/dalton_ga/) [capture].
- **Consent.** Up to the list there was no consent checkbox, call or text consent, automated-dialing wording, "not a condition of purchase" line or named seller; the walker searched each step's text [walk]. No consent-certificate script was detected, and Terms and Privacy were not captured, so arbitration and data-sharing terms are unknown [capture].
- **Data.** Captured pages load FullStory session replay, MNTN retargeting, Optimizely, HubSpot and Tidio, and the footer offers "Do not sell my information" [capture]. Whether these run in checkout was not recorded, and the first checkout URL carries the ZIPs and dates [capture; walk /checkoutv2/moveitems/].
- **Phone.** A phone link leads all six pages whose mobile fold order was recorded, and the number varies by page, viewport and session, which suggests call tracking [capture]. "Call our Moving Specialists" appears on / and city pages, while /how-it-works/ promises "No spam, no surprises" and real humans "by phone or chat" [capture].
- **Partners and payment.** The footer links "Porch Group" and lists four brands under "PARTNERS" [capture]. Pages say consumers "pay only when the job is done" (/how-it-works/), while v1 said the card is charged a day before; the two may describe different steps [capture; v1 §2.4].

## SEO and content
- **Scale.** About 125,334 visits and 80,203 keywords a month, worth about $10.45 per visit site-wide, against $5.60 for move.org and $6.24 for moving.com [semrush domain_rank]. Authority Score is 48, with 11,736 referring domains; 96% of backlinks are nofollow, so compare authority by referring domains [semrush backlinks_overview]. It is a benchmark, not a near-term target [inference].
- **City pages.** Ten of the top 15 pages are city pages, with 30,820 visits (24.6%) [semrush top_pages]. The Dallas page is on page one for "movers dallas" and "moving companies dallas", beside Yelp, Thumbtack and Forbes [semrush phrase_organic_top10]. keyword-research §1 says city pages "drive most traffic", but the data show only the largest family in the top 15, and with 57% of traffic outside it, a majority cannot be shown [semrush].
- **Few-keyword pages.** Tallahassee, Beckley and Oshkosh earn about 140-160 visits per keyword, against about 7 for Los Angeles [semrush top_pages]. One or two big, possibly non-local queries may drive each, so check them before citing small metros as "achievable" (keyword-research §2) [inference].
- **Template.** The three captured city pages share their first 12 H2s bar one, a meta template and repeated intro paragraphs; what clearly differs is the mover inventory [capture]. Cost sections, FAQ answers and the local advice section (mover-sourced on two pages, a HireAHelper guide on Tallahassee) were not captured in full [capture].
- **Other drivers.** The #2 page, /services/moving-companies/ (7,969 visits), is a national service pillar, not the "best of" guide keyword-research §2 files it as [semrush top_pages; capture]. U-Haul and PODS cost guides and a containers guide are the only content pages in the top 15, at 6.1% of traffic [semrush top_pages].
- **Long distance.** No route, state or long-distance URL is in its top 15, and its long-distance offer is labor at both ends, though /services/moving-companies/ links "See our long-distance movers" (not captured) [semrush; capture /services/]. That supports AMC's long-distance and route waves (site-architecture §5) [inference].
- **Profiles and markup.** Profile titles and H1s target city terms and frame customer ratings as "HireAHelper's Review Of" the company [capture profiles]. Their MovingCompany markup carries a rating equal to the visible one (5.0/10; 4.9/827), which appears to fit Google's pattern for reviews of other businesses (compliance facts §5) [capture]. Markup on its own pages is only partly visible [capture].
- **Content.** /advice/ shows bylines, dates and answer-first excerpts; no article page was captured [capture /advice/]. The calculator asks for no contact details and publishes a method, but says "not estimates" under an "Accurate Moving Estimate" title, and it ranks in neither the top 15 pages nor the top 10 for "moving cost calculator" [capture /moving-cost-calculator/; semrush].

## Design and UX
On mobile, the homepage opens with phone, logo and Trustpilot rating, then the H1 and two service-choice buttons, below a promo-code banner [capture /]. Desktop navigation includes a "Why Book with HireAHelper?" trust page [capture /]. Mobile profiles show no quote form among the first recorded elements, and two images near the top of / have empty alt text [capture]. City cards stack rating, reviews, base city, moves booked and the mover's own text, then hourly terms and "Select Movers" [capture /movers/dalton_ga/]. The homepage chooser is a custom widget the walker could not operate [walk /].

## What AMC should adopt or adapt
- **Value before contact** [walk]. Better: after move details and the coverage pre-check, preview how many Approved movers serve the move, with names, USDOT numbers, verification dates and review summaries, but no prices. Ask for a date or flexible range with nothing pre-filled, keep any save-progress email optional and apart from consent, and reword the planned "See available movers" button, which echoes a HireAHelper phrase. Serves: the quote survey (lead-lifecycle §2 steps 3 and 5; visual-direction §5.3, §13.7).
- **Consumer choice** [capture /how-it-works/]. Better: show the named eligible movers and let the consumer deselect any before consent, within the company-count limit; explain the order and label paid placement "Sponsored"; name the company first when a request starts on its profile. Serves: named-mover consent (consent copy §5; compliance facts §2-§4; mover-verification §5).
- **A plain first question** [capture /; walk /]. Better: one first question in native, accessible controls with 44px targets, AMC's role line and one dated proof line near the H1, a footer "How we make money" link, and "How we verify movers" in the main navigation. Serves: the mobile survey start and trust pages (visual-direction §12; consent copy §1, §7).
- **Published vetting checklist** [capture /how-it-works/]. Better: one canonical page listing AMC's checks (USDOT, household-goods authority, no broker authority, insurance filings, state credentials, weekly rechecks), summarized beside every badge, with "verified on {date}" and FMCSA links on each profile, and every "licensed" claim, including the draft role statement's, tied to what was checked. Serves: movers-only and Preferred Movers (mover-verification §3-§5; consent copy §8) and "usdot number lookup" demand (4,400 a month) [semrush phrase_these].
- **Reviews with replies** [capture]. Better: AMC's labels (Verified platform inquiry, Verified customer, Verified completed move), never "paid move" or a mover-confirmed label; platform-sent invitations; a portal reply tool; featured reviews chosen by a published neutral rule. Serves: reviews and reputation (mover-verification §6; compliance facts §4).
- **Profile and card anatomy** [capture]. Better: evidenced fields only (verification date, review counts by label, member since, last review date), job counts only with AMC evidence, no placeholders, "based in" apart from "serves this area", "Verified by AMC" apart from "Company says", neutral generated meta, USDOT in markup, prices only from mover-published rate cards after counsel review, and a request button that opens the survey for that mover's area, names it first and keeps normal sharing rules. Serves: Preferred Movers profiles (mover-verification §5; site-architecture §6.3, §8.2, §8.5).
- **Inventory-led city pages** [capture; inference]. Better: count only Approved movers with explicit county or ZIP coverage, publish only past G1, keep shared explainers on one canonical page, define how repeated mover cards count toward G7, and use mover tips only when attributed, moderated and fact-checked. Serves: the no-doorway rule (site-architecture §4.1; compliance facts §5).
- **Pillars, calculator and bylines** [capture; semrush]. Better: national service pillars with a definition, inclusions, steps and vetting; an ungated calculator showing ranges, never estimates, with a dated method that says which dataset drives which output; named reviewers, sources and author pages. Serves: SEO content (site-architecture §1.2, §6.3, §7.2-§7.4).
- **One source of truth** [capture]. Better: every count and rating from one data service with source and "as of" date, third-party ratings kept apart, and one canonical support number in the footer, confirmation page and markup. Serves: trust and entity consistency (mover-verification §6-§7; site-architecture §7.1).
- **Provider tools** [capture footer]. Better: CRM, billing and training in the company portal, with lead prices and credit rules published on /for-movers/. Serves: the company portal and lead marketplace (shared-pricing-and-credits.md).

## What AMC should avoid
- Booking, payment, "book" wording and claims that depend on them (compliance facts §2; discovery brief §1) [capture; inference].
- Guarantees, coverage claims and price promises; explain released value and full value protection neutrally, with FMCSA links (compliance facts §1) [capture].
- Review thresholds inside the badge, and "independently" unless a third party checks (mover-verification §4) [capture /how-it-works/, /about/].
- A specialists line that helps pick a mover, phone-first headers, promo banners and no-spam absolutes (consent copy §6; compliance facts §2) [capture].
- "Best" in templated headings and out-of-area movers in city counts (site-architecture §4.1, §8.2) [capture].
- Superlative or first-person template meta, "review of" titles and unedited mover claims (mover-verification §5) [capture].
- Availability claims and pre-filled dates (lead-lifecycle §2-§3) [capture; walk].
- Unmasked session replay or ad pixels on survey, contact and consent screens, and move details in URLs; replay on forms is a counsel question (site-architecture §1.2) [capture; inference].
- Co-branded entry points without AMC's role line, consent and disclosure, and sharing survey data with partner brands without separate consent (consent copy §3, §7) [capture footer].
- DIY cost guides in Wave 1, FAQPage markup and rating markup on AMC's own pages (keyword-research §3; compliance facts §5) [semrush; capture].

## Gaps and open questions
- Booking and checkout: where name, phone and payment are first asked, and any consent or arbitration wording [walk].
- Terms, Privacy, guarantee, "Complaints & Claims" and "License & Insurance" pages, /affiliate/ and partner pages [capture].
- Provider fees and onboarding, and whether fees or paid tiers shape "Best Match", city order or featured reviews [capture].
- HireAHelper's own FMCSA status: a third-party review lists USDOT 3214531, not checked against SAFER [search: goodmigrations.com, third party]. Whether providers' USDOT numbers and state licenses are verified or shown [capture].
- Ownership: search results report a 2019 Porch Group purchase, not re-checked [search: geekwire.com, third party]. The evidence pack's "sister brands" wording is not in the capture, which heads the four brands "PARTNERS" [capture].
- BBB and regulator records were not confirmed; searches that surfaced no action prove nothing [search].
- Review dates and moderation; full JSON-LD, canonicals and state hubs; keyword-level Semrush data; AI-engine answers [capture; semrush].
- Owner decisions: pricing a request submitted from a mover's own AMC profile; matching labor-only requests between two areas, which AMC's rules class as interstate (mvp-scope §6; lead-lifecycle §3); the state crumb when a city passes its gate but its state hub does not (site-architecture §3.3) [inference].
