# HireAHelper (company profile and marketplace): reference review

A marketplace for booking hourly moving crews and local full-service movers, paid through its own checkout [capture /how-it-works/, /services/]. It is on the list because the owner pointed to one of its profiles, a structure AMC's discovery brief calls "the best model" (00-discovery-brief.md §3 item 4).

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-27. **Evidence:** 13 of 14 pages captured (/affiliate/ blocked by Cloudflare); one automated walk from the A-1 Moving profile to the mover list, while the homepage walk stalled on a custom widget; Semrush US, October 2026; standard WebSearch, partly not re-checked. **Confidence:** medium, because the profile, city pages and pre-contact flow are well captured, but booking, checkout consent, the terms and provider fees were not seen.

## At a glance
- Consumers compare providers, book one team and pay through HireAHelper [capture /, /how-it-works/].
- Two short screens from a profile reach the mover list with no contact details; the email box there is optional [walk /checkoutv2/helperlist/].
- No consent wording appeared before that list, and booking was not walked [walk].
- Its best trust signals (paid-move reviews, booked-move counts, the $1,000 guarantee, the advocate role) depend on running the booking and taking payment, which AMC does not do [inference]. That revises the discovery brief's "best model" view.
- Vetting is published, but the excerpts show no USDOT, MC or license check and no last-checked date [capture /how-it-works/, profiles].
- Counts and ratings differ between pages and carry no date [capture].
- About 125,334 estimated organic visits a month, with city pages the largest family [semrush domain_rank, top_pages].

- The dates came pre-filled: nothing was typed, yet 11/3/2026 reached the list header [walk]. Pages promise movers "actually available on your date", so a date the consumer never chose can shape the list [capture /services/moving-companies/; inference].
- The consumer picks one team, which implies no broadcast [capture /how-it-works/; inference]. "Best Match" is the first sort option, and the "How the match works" block was cut off, so whether fees affect the order is unknown [walk; capture /how-it-works/].
- The Georgia-to-Florida test ran as "No Truck, Labor Only" and listed other companies first, but the form offers only labor and the ZIPs were outside A-1's area, so neither result is conclusive [walk].

## Mover side
Footer links invite movers to get listed, sign up, and use provider software and training [capture footer]. Each booking goes to one chosen team, so there is no sharing cap to manage [inference]. Providers "advertise" on the site, but no captured page says what they pay, and fee figures found in search were not re-checked, so none is repeated [capture /; search: sidehusl.com, third party]. The footer links "Porch Group" and lists four brands under "PARTNERS" [capture footer]. Third parties report a 2019 Porch purchase, not re-checked here [search: geekwire.com, third party].

## Trust, verification and reviews
- **Vetting.** A "five-point vetting process" comes before the first booking; three points were captured: an owner background check, verified business and payment details, and "10+ reviews and a 4-star rating" [capture /how-it-works/]. Full-service bookings also need an active insurance certificate [capture /movers/].
- **What is missing.** The excerpts show no USDOT, MC or state-license check and no last-checked date, though two vetting points and the "License & Insurance" page were not captured [capture]. Profile "Credentials" list only a self-declared uniformed-crew attribute, and "Licensed & insured crews" names no license [capture profiles, /services/moving-companies/].
- **Badge.** The review threshold sits inside "Vetted & Verified", so a business with no reviews cannot qualify [capture /how-it-works/, profiles]. Reviews come only from paid bookings, so where the required pre-booking reviews come from is not said [capture /movers/].
- **Reviews.** "Every review on HireAHelper comes from a real, paid move" [capture /movers/]. Profiles show unedited reviews with mover replies, even to critical ones [capture /movers/los-angeles_ca/excellent-movers-los-angeles/]. City pages feature reviews by no stated rule, and review dates were not captured [capture /movers/dalton_ga/].
- **Guarantees.** One $1,000 promise is "damage coverage" on / and a "Service Guarantee" elsewhere, and the price guarantee narrows to the same mover and date "on our platform" [capture /, /services/moving-companies/, /moving-cost-calculator/].
- **Numbers.** Network size reads "1,700+" (/) and "1848" (/movers/); ratings cite "9,100+" and "13k" reviews and an unsourced "4.8/5" (/about/); one mover's counts differ between its profile and city page; none is dated [capture].
- **Profiles and city pages.** A-1 shows "Up to 0 miles", and both profiles share a "top-rated" meta template in the mover's voice and a "Moving with with" heading [capture profiles]. "Compare the Best 10 Vetted Movers in Tallahassee, FL" counts movers based in Orlando and Georgia [capture /movers/tallahassee_fl/]. v1 saw 7 Dalton movers; the capture shows 20 [v1 §2.4; capture /movers/dalton_ga/].

## Disclosure and consent
- **Role.** The clearest line sits in body copy: "a national marketplace for booking local, vetted movers online" [capture /services/]. No role statement appeared in the flow, and no footer link explains how HireAHelper makes money [walk; capture].
- **Consent.** Before the list there was no consent box, call or text consent, "not a condition of purchase" line or named seller; the walker searched each step [walk]. No consent-certificate script loaded, and Terms and Privacy were not captured [capture].
- **Data.** Captured pages load FullStory session replay, MNTN retargeting and Optimizely, beside a footer "Do not sell my information" link [capture]. Whether these run in checkout is unknown, and the first checkout URL carries the ZIPs and dates [walk].
- **Phone.** A phone number leads all six recorded mobile screens and varies by page and session, which suggests call tracking [capture].
- **Payment timing.** Pages say "pay only when the job is done", while v1 said the card is charged a day before [capture /how-it-works/; v1 §2.4].

## SEO and content
- **Scale.** About 125,334 visits and 80,203 keywords a month, worth $10.45 per visit site-wide; 11,736 referring domains, with 96% of backlinks nofollow [semrush domain_rank, backlinks_overview].
- **City pages.** Ten of the top 15 pages are city pages, with 30,820 visits (24.6%), led by Los Angeles at 8,915 [semrush top_pages]. The Dallas page ranks #8 for "movers dallas" beside Yelp and Thumbtack [semrush phrase_organic_top10]. keyword-research §1 says city pages "drive most traffic", but the data show only the largest family in the top 15, with 57% of traffic outside it [semrush].
- **Few-keyword pages.** Tallahassee, Beckley and Oshkosh earn 140-160 visits per keyword, against 7 for Los Angeles, so check their queries before citing them as proof that small metros are "achievable" (keyword-research §2) [semrush top_pages; inference].
- **Template.** The three captured city pages share their first 12 H2s, bar one on Tallahassee, plus one meta template and repeated intros; only the mover cards clearly differ, and FAQ answers were not captured [capture].
- **Other pages.** The #2 page, /services/moving-companies/ (7,969 visits), is a service pillar, not the "best of" guide keyword-research §2 files it as [semrush top_pages; capture]. U-Haul and PODS cost guides and a containers guide are the only content pages in the top 15 [semrush top_pages].
- **Long distance.** HireAHelper appears in none of six measured long-distance, interstate or route top 10s, which leaves AMC's planned route and long-distance pages without this rival [semrush phrase_organic_top10; inference].
- **Markup.** Profiles carry MovingCompany markup whose rating matches the page; markup on its own pages is only partly visible [capture].
- **Content.** /advice/ shows bylines, dates and answer-first excerpts [capture /advice/]. The calculator asks for no contact details and publishes a method, but says "not estimates" under an "Accurate Moving Estimate" title [capture /moving-cost-calculator/].

## Design and UX
On mobile, the homepage leads with phone, logo and Trustpilot rating, then the H1 and two large service buttons, and a promo banner tops the page [capture /]. Mobile profiles show no quote form among the first recorded elements [capture profiles]. City cards stack rating, reviews, base city, moves booked and the mover's own text, then hourly terms and "Select Movers" [capture /movers/dalton_ga/].

## What AMC should adopt or adapt
- **Value before contact** [walk]. Better: after move details and the coverage check, preview matched movers (names, USDOT, verification dates, reviews) without prices; ask for a date or range, never pre-filled; keep any save-progress email apart from consent; reword the planned "See available movers" button, which echoes HireAHelper. Serves the survey (lead-lifecycle §2; visual-direction §5.3).
- **Consumer choice** [capture /how-it-works/]. Better: show the named movers, allow deselection within the company-count limit, explain the order, and label paid placement. Serves named-mover consent (consent draft §5; compliance facts §2-§4).
- **Role line** [capture /services/]. Better: AMC's role statement beside the hero button and in the survey, a footer "How we make money" link, and a "licensed" claim linked to what AMC checked. Serves trust pages (consent draft §1, §7).
- **Vetting checklist** [capture /how-it-works/]. Better: one page with the exact checks, recheck cadence and limits, linked from every badge, with dated FMCSA links on profiles and no "independent" claim unless a third party checks. Serves Preferred Movers and "usdot number lookup" (4,400/mo) (mover-verification §3-§5) [semrush phrase_these].
- **Reviews with replies** [capture]. Better: AMC's evidence labels, never "paid move" or a mover-confirmed label; platform-sent invitations; a portal reply tool; neutral featuring rules. Serves reviews (mover-verification §6).
- **Profile and card anatomy** [capture]. Better: evidenced fields only, job counts only where AMC holds evidence, no placeholders, "based in" apart from "serves", verified fields apart from "company says", neutral meta, USDOT in markup, and a request button naming that mover first under normal sharing rules. Serves profiles (mover-verification §5; site-architecture §8.2, §8.5).
- **Inventory-led city pages** [inference]. Better: count only Approved movers with explicit coverage, pass G1, keep shared explainers on one page, and define how repeated mover cards count toward G7. Serves the no-doorway rule (site-architecture §4.1).
- **Pillar, calculator and bylines** [capture]. Better: service pillars on this structure; ranges, never estimates, with a dated method; named reviewers and sources. Serves SEO (site-architecture §1.2, §6.3, §7.2-§7.4).
- **One source of truth** [capture]. Better: dated, sourced counts from one data service, and one support number in the footer and markup. Serves trust and entity data (site-architecture §7.1).

## What AMC should avoid
- Booking, payment, "book" wording and claims built on them (compliance facts §2; discovery brief §1) [capture].
- Guarantees and price promises; explain valuation options neutrally instead (compliance facts §1) [capture].
- Ratings inside the badge (mover-verification §4) [capture].
- Phone-first headers, promo banners, no-spam absolutes and an advice line that helps pick a mover, which could look like arranging moves (consent draft §6; compliance facts §2) [capture].
- "Best" and out-of-area movers in city counts (site-architecture §4.1) [capture].
- Template superlatives, mover-voice meta, "our review of" headings and unedited mover claims (mover-verification §5) [capture].
- Availability claims and pre-filled dates (lead-lifecycle §2-§3) [walk].
- Replay and ad scripts on survey and consent screens, and move details in URLs (site-architecture §1.2) [capture; inference].
- Sharing survey data with partner brands without separate consent (consent draft §3) [capture footer].
- DIY cost guides in Wave 1, and FAQPage or self-rating markup (keyword-research §3; compliance facts §5) [semrush; capture].
