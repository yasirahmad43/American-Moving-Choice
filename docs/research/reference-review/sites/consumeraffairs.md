# ConsumerAffairs: Best Long-Distance Moving Companies: reference review

A multi-industry review publisher with a paid partner program; the owner listed its long-distance ranking, which teaches AMC about disclosure, ranking method and consent more than moving SEO [search: /about/faq/; semrush].

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-27. **Evidence:** capture blocked (bot protection, HTTP 403); no walks; Semrush US, October 2026; WebSearch; v1 §2.10. Paths are ConsumerAffairs' own pages seen in search summaries; "LD page" is the owner's URL; "BBB" is the Tulsa BBB profile (third party). **Confidence:** low, because no page or flow was observed and many search details were not re-run.

## At a glance
- Businesses pay to join its Authorized Partner program, and it may also earn on clicks, forms and calls [search: /about/faq/]. It does not present itself as a mover or broker; its FMCSA status was not checked [search].
- Guides say partner pay does not affect picks; the FAQ says it may affect who is featured and in what order [search: /movers/tulsa.html, /about/faq/].
- Call and text consent sits in the sitewide Terms; the form was never seen [search: /about/terms_of_use/; capture].
- Nothing shows the consumer seeing, naming or limiting companies before submitting [search; capture].
- "Verified" reviews rest on contact details, not bookings [search: /online/consumeraffairs.html].
- /movers/ earns about 5.6% of the domain's 420,319 estimated monthly visits, and the LD page under 188 [semrush: domain_rank, top_pages_more].

Its examples are home services, and no moving-specific eligibility checks, prices, caps or exclusivity terms were found [search].

## Trust, verification and reviews
- **Paid marker.** v1's blue check for paid partners was not re-confirmed [v1 §2.10; search]. An industry self-regulatory program (ERSP) reportedly found paid ties under-disclosed, and a banner marking paying companies followed; source and date were not pinned [search: Wikipedia, truthinadvertising.org, third party].
- **Authority type.** The top long-distance pick, International Van Lines, is labeled a "broker and carrier hybrid", and Condor a carrier; these are the site's labels, not FMCSA records [search; inference]. No pure broker appeared in the ranking [search]. A Safe Ship review page earns about 511 visits a month, and another reference calls Safe Ship a broker [semrush: top_pages_more; v1 §2.1].
- **Deposits.** The LD page gives typical deposits per pick, and guides say brokers often take 20 to 50% upfront, carriers much less [search: LD page]. Per-card USDOT numbers were not re-confirmed [search].
- **Reviews.** Contact details confirm a real person, backed by software and moderators; nothing ties a review to a move [search: /online/consumeraffairs.html]. Pages credit a "ConsumerAffairs Research Team", and no named authors surfaced [search: /movers/].
- **Method.** Three pillars, "verified feedback submitted to ConsumerAffairs between March 1, 2023, and Feb. 28, 2026", and one Chicago-to-Kansas City price scenario, under a 2026 title [search: LD page; v1 §2.10]. City guides weight local reviews 65% and national record 22% [v1 §2.10].
- **Drift.** Dallas names Condor Moving Systems "best value" at 1.8 from 100 reviews, while other pages show other figures [search: /movers/texas/dallas/]. Review pages paginate with ?page= under rotating titles [search: /movers/condor.html].
- **Other.** The containers guide labels its "Customers Say" summaries AI-generated [search: /movers/moving-containers/]. Tulsa names PODS, a container service, best overall among 37 companies [search: /movers/tulsa.html].
- **Record.** Nemet Chevrolet (4th Cir. 2009) dismissed review claims under Section 230, and Consumer Cellular (D. Or. 2016) denied the site's anti-SLAPP motion [search: blog.ericgoldman.org, third party]. Icon Health & Fitness alleged that paying companies get favorable treatment, and a Law360 headline reports a HomeSafe pay-to-play suit; outcomes were not checked [search: blog.ericgoldman.org, law360.com, third party].

## Disclosure and consent
- **Two statements.** Guides say picks "may be Authorized Partners that compensate us, but this does not affect our recommendations" [search: /movers/tulsa.html]. The FAQ says pay "may influence which partners we feature and the order in which they appear", but not editors' advice [search: /about/faq/]. On a ranked list, readers cannot tell which applies [inference]. Above-the-fold disclosure is unknown [capture].
- **Consent.** The Terms' summary includes consent to direct mail, automated calls, texts and call recording [search: /about/terms_of_use/]. v1 found that submitting counts as consent to autodialed or prerecorded contact from ConsumerAffairs or the matched brand [v1 §2.10]. No "not a condition of purchase" wording surfaced [search].
- **Disputes.** The Terms require binding JAMS arbitration, or small claims court in Douglas County, Nevada, after 60 days of negotiation, with no class actions and a one-year claim limit [search: /about/terms_of_use/].
- **Complaints.** BBB shows an A+ rating, accreditation since 10/12/2016 and 7 complaints in 3 years, describing unwanted calls after the questionnaire [search: BBB, as indexed]. The reply says details go only to "the specific partners you've given us permission to connect you with" [search: BBB].
- **Operator.** Consumers Unified, LLC runs it; Zac Carman bought it in 2010, and it is based in Tulsa [search: urlert.com, Wikipedia, third party]. BBB also lists a Stateline, Nevada address, in the Terms' venue county [search: bbb.org; inference].

## SEO and content
- **Scale.** /movers/ draws about 23.7k visits, behind whole-domain HireAHelper (125,334), Move.org (107,657), Moving.com (64,797), moveBuddha (38,335) and MyGoodMovers (25,778) [semrush: domain_rank, top_pages_more]. Moving pages outside /movers/ add an unmeasured amount [search: /homeowners/, /moving/reviews/].
- **Mix.** The top 25 pages hold 66.75% of the folder (15,866 visits). Relocation lists, storage, address-change and how-to pages take about 44%, the cost guide 10.2%, and the hub plus two brand pages about 13% [semrush: top_pages_more, computed].
- **Head terms.** No ConsumerAffairs URL is in Google's top 10 for "best long distance moving companies" (49,500/mo, KD 36), "long distance moving companies", "interstate movers" or "cross country movers"; US News leads [semrush: SERP and keyword files]. The hub, itself a best-of guide, ranks #6 for "best interstate moving companies", and the cost guide (2,429 visits) #6 for "how much do movers cost", under Reddit [semrush; search: /movers/].
- **Geo.** City and state titles use a "4 Best" template, from Texas to New York City [search: /movers/texas/, /movers/new-york/new-york/]. None ranks in three sampled local top 10s, where carriers, directories, Forbes Advisor and forums do [semrush: SERP files].
- **URLs.** Geo pages use folders; other pages mix .html, .htm, an underscore and flat city guides; profiles sit in /movers/ and /homeowners/ [semrush: top_pages_more; search]. Best-of pages and /awards/ overlap and reward the same companies [search: /awards/2025/movers/, /movers/tulsa.html].
- **Figures.** Long-distance costs vary by page, source and distance band, unreconciled [search: /movers/how-much-do-movers-cost.html, /movers/cost-to-move-cross-country.html]. State pages give one figure blending local and interstate quotes [search: /movers/connecticut/].
- **AI search.** One unnamed engine listed the LD page second and quoted its picks and disclosure [search: tool answer, single run]. The block did not keep pages out of that index [search; capture].
- **Fit.** This supports AMC's plan: one Wave 1 cost URL, /compare/ held to Wave 2 with no early traffic forecast, and gated geo pages [inference].

## Design and UX
A ZIP field with a time promise opens the funnel, and picks carry "best for" labels [search: /movers/ pages, LD page]. Layout, mobile view, form and JSON-LD were not seen [capture]. The evidence pack records an "Access to this page has been denied" page and HTTP 403, and the raw pack logs a script error; no bypass was tried [capture: LD page].

## What AMC should adopt or adapt
- **Published method** [search: LD page]. Better: one method for every page type, the window beside scores and titles, a changelog, and prices from member rate cards (5+ movers per geography) or cited studies, never AMC lead data. Serves /compare/ and /review-methodology/ (site-architecture §4.6, §6.3, §7.3).
- **Authority labels** [search]. Better: classify from FMCSA records, exclude only on recorded dual registration, decide on hybrids before /compare/, and publish "carrier authority only". Serves movers-only (mover-verification §1, §3).
- **Facts before contact** [search: LD page]. Better: USDOT, MC, authority type, check date and FMCSA link per card, plus a new mover-reported deposit field shown neutrally with FMCSA guidance. Serves profiles (visual direction §9.5, §13.6; compliance facts §2).
- **Answer the complaint** [search: BBB]. Better: a coverage check before contact, the unpreselected company-count question, movers named at consent and repeated on confirmation and first email, and the record kept. Serves consent (lead lifecycle §2, §6; consent draft §6 needs this change).
- **ZIP-first entry** [search]. Better: prefill the survey, promise a time only if measured, and use "best for" only with a published method. Serves the survey (visual direction §9.2, §13.2–§13.4).
- **Hub with substance** [semrush]. Better: mover counts by state, how matching and the sharing limit work, and method links. Serves /movers/ (site-architecture §1.2).
- **Original geo data** [search: state pages]. Better: non-price facts from AMC data (n ≥ 30, small cells suppressed); costs from rate cards or studies. Serves geo pages (site-architecture §6.3, §7.5).
- **Sales feedback** [search: brands page]. Better: Booked and Completed feed quality metrics and AMC-sent review invitations, never matching; publish eligibility, caps and credits. Serves the portal (compliance facts §2; mover-verification §6; shared-pricing §9).
- **Labeled AI summaries** [search]. Better: a written policy with labels, proportionate negatives and source links. Serves reviews (mover-verification §6; compliance facts §4).
- **Targeted bot rules** [capture]. Better: challenge only the quote form, allowlist verified search and answer agents, and track ConsumerAffairs in the AI baseline. Serves AI visibility (lead lifecycle §4; site-architecture §7.6, §7.7).

## What AMC should avoid
- Two disclosure versions. Use one counsel-approved statement at quote buttons and consent, not only an FAQ; payment never changes verification, ratings, review display or rating order, and paid slots say "Sponsored" (consent draft §1, §7; mover-verification §4).
- Check marks for paid status; use the rectangular badge with an adjacent explainer, and ask counsel about lead-fee disclosure near ratings (visual direction §9.4; compliance facts §4).
- Consent in the Terms. A separate unticked box names the movers and says consent is not a condition of purchase. Split it from Terms acceptance, which draft §5 bundles, and return §5 to counsel; keep arbitration and call-recording notices out (compliance facts §3).
- A phone step that picks or "assigns" a mover (compliance facts §2).
- A bare "verified" and team bylines; name authors and the still-missing reviewer (mover-verification §6; site-architecture §7.2).
- Fixed-count geo lists and parallel award pages; show real counts above gate G1, one URL per intent (site-architecture §3.1, §4.1).
- Drifting ratings, labels a rating contradicts, and stale year titles; use one record, one cost dataset and CI checks (site-architecture §6.3, §7.3, §9).
- Mixed URL formats and ?page= pagination; use /movers/new-york/new-york-city/ (site-architecture §2.2, §2.4).
- Low-intent storage and address-change families [inference], and containers in mover lists (mover-verification §1).
- Ratings open to pay-to-play claims; publish the formula, log changes, and ask counsel about these cases (compliance facts §4).
- Sizing AMC from the domain total; fix discovery brief §3 and keyword-research §1, and match entity details everywhere (site-architecture §7.1).
