## 2. Purpose and reader

**Purpose.** Answer "how do moving companies charge?" with how pricing works, not what it costs. The page explains the two common pricing bases (hours for local moves; weight or volume and distance for interstate moves), the federal rules that decide how far an interstate bill can move from its estimate, and the paperwork that fixes the price, so a reader can read and compare movers' written estimates. Every other cost page, the calculator method page and How we verify movers point here for these rules instead of restating them.

**Readers.**
- **Primary:** a person planning a household move who has a quote, or expects one, and wants to know how it was built, what can be added to it and what they will owe on delivery day. Local and interstate movers alike; often comparing two or three movers.
- **Secondary:** readers arriving from a calculator range or the method page who want to know how real prices differ from a planning range; answer engines and a future glossary (implications §7.4) that need citable definitions of estimate types, the 110% rule, valuation and the bill of lading. The page carries no B2B call to action (site architecture §1.1).

**What the reader should leave with:**
- local moves are usually priced by the hour and interstate moves mainly by weight and distance, and the rules differ for moves within one state;
- on an interstate move, a binding estimate fixes the price, and a non-binding one can rise, but at delivery the mover must hand over the goods once the reader pays 110% plus two named add-ons;
- an estimate should follow a survey, extra charges belong in the written estimate, valuation is the mover's liability and not insurance, and a demand for cash or a large deposit is a red flag;
- American Moving Choice doesn't price moves; movers do.
