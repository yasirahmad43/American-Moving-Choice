## 2. Purpose and reader

**Purpose.** This is the one home (content/README.md §9) for the carrier and broker definitions, the comparison table, reading an FMCSA record, broker disclosure duties, why American Moving Choice (AMC) is movers-only and why it holds no operating authority. Implications §7.1 item 1 lists it among the first trust pages, and §7.4 puts it first among comparison pages ("a table plus how to check any company's FMCSA record"). Mover verification §7 asks for a "Movers vs Brokers" explainer. How we verify movers links here for definitions and for reading an FMCSA record, and How AMC makes money links here right after ST-1a.

**Readers.**
- **First:** a consumer who searched "movers vs brokers", "moving broker" or "what is a moving broker", often holding a quote, who wants to know which kind of company they're dealing with, what changes if it's a broker, and how to check.
- **Second:** a consumer on AMC who saw "Movers only, no brokers" and wants to know what it means, why AMC chose it and what AMC itself is.
- **Also:** people who plan to use a broker (part of the "moving broker" intent). The page describes brokers fairly and gives them the checks for one. Answer engines quoting definitions; the owner and counsel, who see every AMC claim about brokers and AMC's own role in one place.

**What the reader should leave knowing.** A mover carries out the move and is responsible for it; a broker arranges it with a carrier. How the two differ on authority, filings, estimates, moving day and claims. How a broker must identify itself. How to read SAFER and Licensing and Insurance, and why an MC number or the look of a number proves nothing. What to ask any company. That brokers are legal, why AMC still excludes them (dual registrations included), how van-line agents fit, what AMC does and doesn't do, and how to report a broker found through AMC.
