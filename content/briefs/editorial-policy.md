## 2. Purpose and reader

**Purpose.** Publish the editorial policy that site architecture §7.2 requires ("editorial policy (including how AI assistance is used and checked)") and that implications §7.1 lists among the first trust pages ("an Editorial policy that covers AI use"). The page owns authors and reviewers, sources, numbers and dates, corrections, AI use, images and editorial independence (content README §9). Every review note on a guide, cost, route, location and moving-to page points here (site architecture §7.2; the disclosure default in §8), the calculator method page sends error reports here, and the footer lists it (visual direction §9.10). README §2's front-matter comment, `ai_assisted: true # see the editorial policy`, makes this the page that explains that flag on every draft.

The page also has to be true of itself. An AI drafting agent wrote it from AMC's documents, and its human check is pending. So the policy allows AI first drafts, requires a named person to check them against the sources and approve them before publication, and discloses AI help in each page's review note, which this page carries too. Under an owner default (§8), the note names the author as the person who edits the AI draft and answers for its content ("Edited by"), so the byline doesn't claim a person wrote what AI drafted.

**Readers.**
1. A consumer on a guide, cost page or location page who clicked the review note or the footer link to find out who wrote the page, whether a mover paid for it and whether AI wrote it. This is the main reader, and the answer has to be in the first screen.
2. A consumer who found an error and wants to report it.
3. Journalists, partners, search quality reviewers and AI answer engines assessing how American Moving Choice (AMC) makes content (site architecture §7.2, §7.7). They need specific statements they can quote.
4. AMC's own authors, reviewers and data owners. The page is the public form of the rules in README §4 to §8 and site architecture §6 and §7.
5. Secondary: a mover asking whether it can buy coverage. The answer is no, and the page carries no B2B link or call to action (site architecture §1.1).

**After reading, the reader can:** say who wrote and reviewed a page and how to check those people; tell where a fact or figure came from and how old it is; say what a mover can't buy; describe what AI does and doesn't do at AMC, and who approved a page; and report an error.

**Not on this page** (README §9 homes): how reviews are collected, checked and rated, and the AI review-summary policy itself (Review methodology); every check on movers (How we verify movers); who pays, how requests are priced and what payment never changes in lists (How AMC makes money); the cost model (How the calculator works).
