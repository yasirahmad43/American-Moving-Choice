## 2. Purpose and reader

**Purpose.** This page is the one home for the money topic: who pays American Moving Choice (AMC), how requests are priced to movers (in brief), AMC's incentives and their limits, what payment never changes, sponsored placement and related parties (content README §9). It carries the role-and-money statement, ST-1a, in full (README §9, last line; site architecture §1.2 Trust pages row, §7.1). Every money surface points here: the ST-1b money line under the first screen's call to action, the header link that cannot be closed, the footer, the consent block and the confirmation page (implications §3.4, §8.4; site architecture §7.1). So the first screen must answer the click on its own.

**Readers.**
1. A consumer in the quote survey who tapped "How AMC makes money" from the money line or the Review and send screen. They want to know whether the site is free, who pays it, and whether that money shapes what they see or what they're asked. They need the answer in the first screen and then a way back to the survey.
2. A consumer reading the directory or a location page who wonders whether listed movers paid for their spot.
3. A reviewer, journalist or AI answer engine checking how AMC is paid (site architecture §7.1, §7.4, §7.7). They need self-contained statements they can quote.
4. Secondary: a mover checking what money can and can't buy. The buying rules live on `/for-movers/pricing/`. This page links there and never says "lead" (README §4).

**After reading, the reader can:** name who pays and for what; see how AMC's revenue varies with their own answer to "How many moving companies would you like to hear from?"; list what money can't change and what AMC doesn't sell; and say who receives their contact details.

**Not on this page** (README §9 homes): how the count question works, naming and removing movers, withdrawal and who else handles details (Which companies receive my request); every check and the badge's meaning (How we verify movers); ratings and moderation (Review methodology); prices and the credit schedule (`/for-movers/pricing/`).

## 4. Information gain

What this page gives a reader that the 11 reference sites did not:

1. **It names the payer and the unit sold.** Movers pay per request bought; the consumer pays nothing. moveBuddha's disclosure names no payer, Move.org's covers clicks, calls and sponsored spots but not requests, and on Moving.com's captured pages only `/leads/` says movers buy leads (matrix §3.1; matrix §5 item 4; implications D4).
2. **It shows how AMC's revenue moves with the consumer's own answer.** No reference lets the consumer set the count, so none could say this (matrix §3.2, §3.8). The `{{pricing.revenue_relationship}}` sentence does (implications §11 Q8).
3. **It publishes the limits on that incentive:** no preselected answer, equal weight, options capped at coverage, and no tests judged on revenue (implications §2.3 items 2 and 4). No reference shows anything like it [cross-site analysis].
4. **It separates billing standing from spend.** Billing standing decides who can be named; spend never affects inclusion or order. MyGoodMovers' Terms let paid plans change listing order, and HomeGuide drops out-of-budget pros from results (matrix §3.1; implications §9.2).
5. **It lists what isn't for sale:** rankings, awards, placement, reviews, editorial coverage and the badge. Move.org's partner page lists rankings and awards under "Editorial opportunities" (matrix §3.1, §5 item 5; implications D13; site architecture §1.1).
6. **It sets sponsored rules before any sponsored product exists**, including the ban on "Preferred", "Featured" and "Partner". Move.org's sponsored slots carry those words (matrix §3.1, §3.4; site architecture §0 item 5).
7. **It discloses related parties and keeps them out of featuring, ranking and comparisons.** Two movers CMR calls "related companies" lead both of its captured lists (matrix §3.1, §5 item 5; implications §4.3).
8. **It makes no "never sell" claim.** Instead it says who receives the details and that they pay. CMR prints "We never sell your info." beside its form (matrix §3.3, §5 item 15; implications §3.7).
9. **It states the badge's money relation:** not sold, and buying more never earns or keeps it. HireAHelper's badge needs reviews, CMR and moveBuddha stack badges, and "Preferred" already reads as paid in this market (matrix §3.4; implications §4.3, §5.1).
10. **It puts the pricing mechanics in front of consumers**, in brief: private per-mover prices, exclusive versus shared, step-down credits. No captured provider page shows prices, caps and refunds together, and none shows anything like shared-request credits (matrix §3.8; implications §9.1).
11. **It uses one statement, word for word, here, in consent, on confirmation and in the footer** (ST-1a). Move.org's money strip can be closed, CMR's disclosure is a separate block a quote can drop, and CMR's disclosure meta and body disagree (matrix §3.1, §5 item 4; implications §3.4; site architecture §7.1).

## 10. Open items

1. **The default revenue sentence overstates one detail.** At shared pricing §5's sample prices, a shared request bought by four movers earns $82.50 (companies A to D), or $88.00 when all four charge $40.00. Both are below the $90.00 exclusive alternative; only five buyers ($100.00) exceed it. "Four" holds only when the exclusive buyer's price is lower, for example company D's $75.00 or B's $80.00. The sentence also says "the sample prices we're testing", but §5 calls them "illustrative prices, not recommendations", and no price test has run. Per stored-texts.md, a variant should be proposed there for owner and counsel review. This draft inserts the current default unchanged.
2. **Site architecture §1.1 says consumer pages carry no lead-pricing talk**, while README §9 gives this page "how requests are priced to movers (in brief)". The draft keeps it to mechanics, with no figures and no B2B call to action, and says "request", never "lead". The SEO owner should record the exception in §1.1.
3. **Copy-check allowances.** "booking fees" appears only negated, as the must-cover asks ("booking" is on the README §5 don't-write list). "Preferred", "Featured" and "Partner" appear only in quotes in the sponsored rules (site architecture §0 item 5). "don't sell" appears in two headings about placement, not data. A check for the banned no-sale line should match "sell your details/data/info", not "don't sell".
4. **"Your contact details go only to movers"** sits beside service providers (hosting, email, text messaging) that also handle details. The Counsel marker asks counsel to confirm the wording with the privacy policy's provider list (implications §3.7).
5. **Credit-funded purchases.** Shared pricing §2 kind 4 allows goodwill and promotional account credit, so a mover can buy a request with credit that no cash backs. The page treats credit as payment ("pays us for your request"). Owner and counsel to confirm that reads accurately.
6. **Related parties are undefined.** No document defines a "tie" or which "people" count (owners, staff, contractors, family), or whether a related mover can hold the badge (implications §4.3). Owner and counsel define these (brief §11 Q1; implications §11 Q10).
