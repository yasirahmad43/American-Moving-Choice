# How do moving companies charge?

Movers, not American Moving Choice, set prices. Local moves are usually charged by the hour. Interstate moves are mainly charged by the shipment's weight and the distance, plus services such as packing. On an interstate move, the type of written estimate, binding or non-binding, decides how far the final bill can move from the estimate.

## Which rules apply to your move?

Two sets of rules shape how a mover can price a move. Which set applies depends on the route, not the distance.

- **Moves between states** follow federal rules from the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA), in 49 CFR Part 375. These rules also cover a move between two places in one state if the shipment passes through another state ([FMCSA: What is an interstate move?](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/consumer-protection/protect-your-move/are-you-moving/what-interstate-move)). Part 375 doesn't apply, though, to a move within a commercial zone, even one that crosses a state line. FMCSA's example is a move from Brooklyn, New York, to Hackensack, New Jersey ([FMCSA handbook, October 2023](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/sites/fmcsa.dot.gov/files/2023-10/FMCSA_R&R_Handbook_Web_v1.pdf)).
- **Moves within one state** follow that state's rules, not Part 375 ([FMCSA: What is an interstate move?](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/consumer-protection/protect-your-move/are-you-moving/what-interstate-move); [FMCSA handbook, October 2023](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/sites/fmcsa.dot.gov/files/2023-10/FMCSA_R&R_Handbook_Web_v1.pdf)).

States register or license movers for moves within their borders, through agencies such as the [Texas Department of Motor Vehicles](https://www.txdmv.gov/sites/default/files/body-files/Motor_Carrier_Handbook.pdf), California's [Bureau of Household Goods and Services](https://bhgs.dca.ca.gov/licensee/hhm_faqs.shtml) and Florida's [Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services](https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Moving-Companies). [Verify a mover](/verify-a-mover/) explains federal and state licenses.

<a id="how-do-movers-charge-for-local-moves"></a>

## How do movers charge for local moves?

Local moves are usually priced by the hour. Practices vary from mover to mover, so compare what each hourly price covers before you compare the prices.

### What sets the price of an hourly move?

- **Crew size.** The hourly rate usually depends on how many movers come.
- **Hours.** The bill depends on how long the job takes, and on when the mover starts and stops the clock.
- **Travel time or a truck fee.** Some movers charge for the time it takes to drive to your home and back. Others charge a flat truck or travel fee instead.
- **Minimums.** Some movers charge for a minimum number of hours, even for a short job.
- **Extra services and charges.** Packing, materials, stairs or a long walk to the truck can add to the bill. See [What extra charges can a mover add?](#what-extra-charges-can-a-mover-add)

**In-state rules apply to local moves.** California, for example, allows movers to charge by the hour for in-state moves of less than 100 miles ([BHGS consumer guide](https://bhgs.dca.ca.gov/consumers/hhm.html)). [Verify: C19 (verified-excerpt); BHGS consumer guide; California Maximum Rate Tariff 4, Items 310, 320 and 390.]

### Questions to ask about an hourly price

- How many movers will you send, and what is the hourly rate for that crew?
- When does the clock start and stop? Does it include driving to my home, between homes and back to your base?
- Do you charge a truck or travel fee, as well as or instead of travel time?
- Is there a minimum number of hours, and how do you count part of an hour?
- What does the hourly rate include, such as blankets, wrapping or taking furniture apart?
- What costs extra, such as packing materials, stairs, a long walk to the truck or a heavy item?
- Will you put the price, and how it can change, in a written estimate?

## How do movers charge for interstate moves?

Interstate moves are mainly priced by how much your shipment weighs and how far it travels, plus extra services. An interstate mover's rates for all of these are in its tariff, the published list of its rates and charges. The mover must make the tariff available for you to inspect if you reasonably ask, and must tell you that it's available before you sign the bill of lading ([49 U.S.C. 13702](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/49/13702); [49 CFR 375.213](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-B/subject-group-ECFR26b58a956b3b018/section-375.213)). [How your shipment is weighed](#how-is-your-shipment-weighed) is covered with estimates below, because it depends on the kind of estimate.

[Verify: C18 and C11 (verified-excerpt); 49 U.S.C. 13702(a)(2), (c); 49 CFR 1310.2; 375.213(b). "Weighs and how far it travels" is an editorial summary; the reviewer confirms it.]

**Hourly pricing on an interstate move.** The federal estimate rules describe estimates built from the shipment's weight or volume and the services it needs ([49 CFR 375.405](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-D/section-375.405)). Whatever unit a mover uses, its rates must be in its tariff ([49 U.S.C. 13702](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/49/13702)). So ask the mover to show the unit and the rate in your written estimate, and ask to see that rate in its tariff. [Verify: C19 and C18 (verified-excerpt); 49 CFR Part 375 subpart D, where searches found no provision on hourly pricing, which is a search result and not proof; 49 U.S.C. 13702(a)(2).] [Counsel: how to describe hourly pricing on interstate moves (consumer-moving-rules note, counsel item 7)]

For price ranges on moves between states, see [long-distance moving costs](/moving-costs/long-distance/).

### Can a mover estimate by volume instead?

Federal rules allow estimates by volume. A mover that estimates by volume and converts the volume to weight must explain the conversion in writing ([49 CFR 375.507](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/375.507)). FMCSA's booklet also notes that some rates are based on a minimum weight or volume, so if your shipment is small, ask whether a minimum applies ([Part 375, Appendix A](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/appendix-A_to_part_375)).

[Verify: C9 (verified-excerpt); 49 CFR 375.507(c), 375.401(e), 375.405; Part 375 Appendix A. The research supports estimates by volume only. Don't turn this into "movers must charge by weight" or "movers may charge by volume"; no weight-only rule was found, which is a search result, not proof.]

**In-state moves can differ.** These volume rules are federal rules for interstate moves; for a move within one state, the state's rules apply. California, for example, bars movers from advertising, quoting or charging a rate for a move within the state that's based on cubic feet or any other unit of volume ([California household movers law book](https://bhgs.dca.ca.gov/laws/hhm_law_book-effective_1-1-25.pdf)). [Verify: A7 (verified-excerpt); Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §19253.1, from the 2025 law book; confirm against the 2026 law book.]

## What kinds of written estimates do movers give?

Before you sign the bill of lading, the contract for an interstate move, the mover must give you a written estimate of the total charges. The estimate must say whether it's binding or non-binding. You both sign it, and you get a dated copy ([49 CFR 375.401](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-D/section-375.401)).

[Verify: C1 (verified-excerpt); 49 CFR 375.401(a), (b), (h). Paragraph letters come from search excerpts.]

| What it means | A fixed price for the goods and services it lists | The mover's reasonably accurate estimate; the final charges come from its tariff |

### Why should a survey come before the estimate?

For an interstate move, the mover must survey your belongings before it gives you a written estimate, whatever the distance, unless you waive the survey in a signed writing before loading. The survey can be in person or virtual. A virtual survey must let the mover clearly identify your belongings by live or recorded video ([49 CFR 375.103](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-A/section-375.103); [375.401](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-D/section-375.401)). Older guides may mention a 50-mile limit on surveys; FMCSA's 2022 rule removed it ([87 FR 24431](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2022/04/26/2022-08808/implementation-of-household-goods-working-group-recommendations)).

FMCSA's red flags include a mover that "doesn't perform an on-site inspection of your household items and gives an estimate over the telephone or online — sight unseen" ([FMCSA: Spot the red flags](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/protect-your-move/red-flags)). Waiving the survey means the mover prices your move without seeing what you're moving. If you'd rather not have movers walk through your home, ask for a video survey instead of waiving it.

C14 (verified-excerpt) for the red-flag quote; check it against the live page.] [Counsel: FMCSA's red-flag page speaks of an on-site inspection, while 375.103 counts video surveys as physical surveys. ### Binding estimates

A binding estimate fixes the total price for the goods and services it lists. At delivery, the mover can collect no more than that amount, plus charges for services you asked for after the bill of lading was issued, plus [impracticable-operations charges](#what-extra-charges-can-a-mover-add) of up to 15% of the other charges due at delivery. It bills any impracticable-operations charges above that limit later ([49 CFR 375.403](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-D/section-375.403); 49 CFR 375.703). The mover may charge you for preparing a binding estimate ([49 CFR 375.401](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-D/section-375.401)).

If you add goods or services that the binding estimate doesn't list, the mover must do one of three things before loading: reaffirm the estimate, prepare a new binding estimate that lists the additions for you to sign, or agree with you in writing to treat the estimate as non-binding. If it loads your belongings without doing any of these, the original binding estimate stands ([49 CFR 375.403](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-D/section-375.403)).

[Verify: C3, C4 and A2 (verified-excerpt); 49 CFR 375.401(b)(1), (i); 375.403; 375.703(a).] [Counsel: wording of the delivery limit on binding estimates, with the 110% rule (implications §7.1)]

### Non-binding estimates

A non-binding estimate is the mover's reasonably accurate estimate of what the move should cost, based on the estimated weight or volume of your shipment, the services you need and the survey of your belongings ([49 CFR 375.405](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-D/section-375.405)). It doesn't bind the mover: the final charges come from the mover's tariff and can be higher than the estimate. The estimate must say so, and it must also say that you won't have to pay more than 110% of it at delivery. The mover can't charge you for a non-binding estimate ([49 CFR 375.401](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-D/section-375.401)). If the charges depend on weight, the mover must [weigh your shipment](#how-is-your-shipment-weighed) before it bills you.

[Verify: C5 and C4 (verified-excerpt); 49 CFR 375.401(b)(2), (d); 375.405; 375.507(a). Confirm whether the sentence basing final charges on actual weight, services and the tariff sits in 375.401 or only in Appendix A before quoting it.] [Counsel: 110% rule wording (implications §7.1)]

### How is your shipment weighed?

If a non-binding estimate depends on weight, the mover must weigh your shipment on a certified scale before it charges you ([49 CFR 375.507 to 375.517](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-E/subject-group-ECFR78413e1c7c06a47/)). It can do this in one of two ways:

- **Origin weigh:** the truck is weighed empty, then again loaded with your belongings.
- **Back weigh:** the truck is weighed loaded, then again empty after your belongings are unloaded.

A shipment of 3,000 pounds or less may instead be weighed on a certified platform or warehouse scale, and each weighing needs a weight ticket ([49 CFR 375.507 to 375.517](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-E/subject-group-ECFR78413e1c7c06a47/)). A binding estimate doesn't depend on weight, so the mover doesn't need to weigh the shipment ([FMCSA handbook, October 2023](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/sites/fmcsa.dot.gov/files/2023-10/FMCSA_R&R_Handbook_Web_v1.pdf)).

[Verify: C8 (verified-excerpt); 49 CFR 375.507, 375.509, 375.511, 375.513, 375.515, 375.517; Part 375 Appendix A; the October 2023 handbook on binding estimates. The section that requires weight tickets wasn't confirmed.]

### What are your rights at the scale?

- You, or whoever is paying for the move, can watch every weighing. The mover must tell you where and when it will happen. If you don't go to the original weighings, you've waived your right to watch them ([49 CFR 375.507 to 375.517](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-E/subject-group-ECFR78413e1c7c06a47/)).
- If the shipment was weighed at the start of the move, you can ask for a reweigh after you learn the billing weight and charges, as long as unloading hasn't begun. The charges are then based on the reweigh. Giving up the right to watch a reweigh must be in writing ([49 CFR 375.507 to 375.517](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-E/subject-group-ECFR78413e1c7c06a47/)).

[Verify: C8 (verified-excerpt); 49 CFR 375.509 to 375.517; C8 notes and 375.515 for the written waiver of a reweigh. The reweigh right is tied to origin weighing; no express reweigh right was found for back-weighed shipments, and Appendix A's line on reweigh charges wasn't retrieved, so the page says nothing about reweigh fees.]

### The 110% rule at delivery

On an interstate move with a non-binding estimate, the mover must hand over your belongings at delivery once you pay up to 110% of the estimate, plus charges for any services you asked for after the bill of lading was issued, plus impracticable-operations charges of up to 15% of the other charges due at delivery ([49 CFR 375.407](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/375.407); 49 CFR 375.703). FMCSA's consumer guidance says that if a mover raises the agreed price after loading, "you are not required to pay more than 100% of the binding estimate or 110% of the non-binding estimate" ([FMCSA](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/consumer-protection/protect-your-move/what-happens-if-my-household-goods-mover-increases-agreed)).

The rest of the bill doesn't go away. If the final charges under the mover's tariff come to more than 110% of the estimate, you still owe the difference, and the mover bills it after delivery. FMCSA's booklet says the mover must wait at least 30 days before billing it ([Part 375, Appendix A](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/appendix-A_to_part_375); [FMCSA handbook, October 2023](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/sites/fmcsa.dot.gov/files/2023-10/FMCSA_R&R_Handbook_Web_v1.pdf)). Impracticable-operations charges above the 15% limit are billed after delivery too ([49 CFR Part 375, subpart D](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-D)).

For example, with no services added after the bill of lading and no impracticable-operations charges: if the final charges come to 150% of a non-binding estimate, you pay 110% of the estimate at delivery, and the mover bills the other 40% later.

If a mover won't deliver unless you pay more than these limits, you can file a complaint with FMCSA through its National Consumer Complaint Database at [nccdb.fmcsa.dot.gov](https://nccdb.fmcsa.dot.gov) or by phone at 1-888-368-7238 ([FMCSA: Complaint Center FAQs](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/consumer-protection/complaint-center-faqs)). FMCSA treats it as a "hostage goods" complaint only while the mover still holds your belongings, and it asks for your written estimate, bill of lading, proof of payment and proof that the mover refused to deliver ([FMCSA: Eligible complaints](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/consumer-protection/eligible-complaints)). [Complaints](/complaints/) explains how to file with FMCSA and state agencies.

<a id="what-extra-charges-can-a-mover-add"></a>

## What extra charges can a mover add?

Beyond the main charge, movers charge for extra services, which the rules call accessorial services. On an interstate move, the mover's tariff sets their prices ([49 U.S.C. 13702](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/49/13702); [49 CFR 375.401](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-D/section-375.401)). Common ones include:

| Charge | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Packing | The mover packing your belongings, and any packing materials it supplies |
| Stair (flight) charge | Carrying items up or down stairs |
| Long carry | Carrying items an unusually long distance between the mover's truck and your home |
| Shuttle | A smaller vehicle, used when the mover's main truck can't reach your home |
| Elevator | Moving items by elevator |

The stair, long-carry and shuttle descriptions follow [FMCSA's glossary](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/protect-your-move/glossary) and [Part 375, Appendix A](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/appendix-A_to_part_375).

[Verify: C18 (verified-excerpt); Part 375 Appendix A glossary and the FMCSA glossary for the flight, long-carry and shuttle definitions. The packing and elevator descriptions are plain-language descriptions, not glossary text.]

**When extra charges must be settled.** Before it prepares the bill of lading, the mover must ask about these services and settle what they'll cost, and the bill of lading takes in every service on the estimate. If the mover didn't settle them, it must still deliver your belongings, and it can bill those extra charges only after 30 days ([49 CFR 375.401](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-D/section-375.401); [375.505](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-E/subject-group-ECFR678dd6dec294449/section-375.505)). Services you ask for after the bill of lading is issued can be charged on top of either kind of estimate ([49 CFR 375.403](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-D/section-375.403); [375.407](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/375.407)).

[Verify: C18, C3 and C6 (verified-excerpt for these parts); 49 CFR 375.401(f), per one excerpt; 375.505(b); 375.403; 375.407.]

**Impracticable-operations charges.** Sometimes conditions at a home make pickup or delivery with the mover's normal truck physically impossible, so the mover needs special equipment or extra labor. The mover's tariff defines these conditions, which the rules call impracticable operations. Their charges can come on top of either kind of estimate, but at delivery the mover can collect no more than 15% of all other charges then due, and it bills the rest later ([49 CFR Part 375, subpart D](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-D)). Ask whether any access charge, such as a shuttle, would be billed as an impracticable operation, because that decides how much of it you'd pay at delivery.

[Verify: A2 (verified-excerpt); 49 CFR 375.403; 375.407(d); 375.703(a), (b); definition paraphrased from Part 375 Appendix A. The research note doesn't say how tariffs classify shuttle service, so the page asks the reader to check (brief open item 7).]

Ask each mover about anything its estimate doesn't mention, such as a heavy or bulky item, or storage if your belongings can't be delivered right away, and get the answer into the written estimate.

**In-state moves:** these are federal rules for interstate moves. For a move within one state, ask the mover how it prices extra services, and check your state's rules.

## What is valuation?

Valuation sets how much the mover must pay if your belongings are lost, damaged or not delivered. It's the mover's liability for your belongings under federal law. An interstate mover offers two levels ([49 U.S.C. 14706](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/49/14706); [49 CFR 375.201](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-B/subject-group-ECFRaf957489d1c5963/section-375.201)):

| | Full value protection | Released value |
|---|---|---|
| When it applies | Automatically, unless you waive it in writing | Only if you waive full value protection in writing |
| What the mover owes for items lost, damaged or not delivered | Their replacement value, up to the value declared for your shipment | No more than 60 cents per pound per article |
| What it costs you | The price set in the mover's tariff | Nothing extra |

Because released value pays by weight, it can pay far less than a light, valuable item is worth ([Part 375, Appendix A](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/appendix-A_to_part_375)).

You make the choice on a valuation statement. The written estimate must include a notice about the mover's liability, and the bill of lading records your choice and any charge for the coverage you choose ([49 CFR 375.401](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-D/section-375.401); [375.505](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-E/subject-group-ECFR678dd6dec294449/section-375.505); [FMCSA: valuation](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/protect-your-move/valuation-insurance)).

List any item worth more than $100 a pound, such as jewelry or silverware, for the mover in writing. If you don't, the mover's liability for it may be limited to $100 a pound per article ([49 CFR 375.203](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-375/subpart-B/subject-group-ECFRaf957489d1c5963/section-375.203)).

FMCSA lists a mover's claim that its insurance covers all your goods among its [red flags](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/protect-your-move/red-flags).

If something is lost or damaged, file a claim with the mover. It can't give you less than 9 months to file one, and within 120 days of receiving your claim it must pay it, decline it or make a firm settlement offer in writing, or tell you in writing why it can't yet ([49 U.S.C. 14706](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/49/14706); [49 CFR 370.9](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-370/section-370.9)). [What documents a mover must give you](/questions/what-documents-should-a-mover-give-me/) explains claims and your other rights.

- whether it wants a deposit, how much and when;
- whether the deposit is refundable, and what happens if you cancel or change dates;
- how the deposit counts toward the final bill;
- which forms of payment it accepts at delivery.

- no written estimate, or a price set only after your belongings are loaded;
- a request to sign blank documents;
- a claim that you have more goods than the mover estimated.

## How do you compare quotes like for like?

Put the movers' written estimates side by side and check the same things on each.

| Compare | What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Estimate type | Binding or non-binding | It decides how far the final bill can move from the estimate |
| Pricing basis | Weight or volume and distance (interstate), or hours (local); the weight, volume or hours assumed; how the mover saw your belongings: in person, by video or not at all | A low guess at weight, volume or hours can make an estimate look lower than the final bill will be |
| Included services | Packing, materials, taking furniture apart and putting it back together, crew size | Two prices compare only when they cover the same work |
| Extra charges | Stairs, long carries, a shuttle, an elevator, heavy or bulky items, storage | Charges the estimate doesn't list can add to the bill |
| Valuation | Full value protection or released value; the value declared; the charge | It decides what the mover owes if something is lost or damaged |
| Payment terms | Deposit, refund and cancellation terms; forms of payment at delivery | It tells you what you pay, when and how |

If one estimate is much lower than the others, ask what it leaves out.
